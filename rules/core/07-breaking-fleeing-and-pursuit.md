# 7. Breaking, Fleeing and Pursuit

This chapter picks up a melee once its Melee Outcome is known (chapter 6). The losers test to
see how badly they are pushed back, then the winners decide whether to chase them. The second
half covers Panic: what makes units lose their nerve away from the melee.

## 1. The Break Test

When a melee is not a draw, **every unit on the losing side** takes a Break Test.

1. Roll 2D6. This is the **natural roll**.
2. Add the difference between the two sides' Melee Outcome totals. This is the **modified
   roll**.
3. Compare both with the unit's **Resolve (Rs)**. A unit whose models have different Resolve
   uses the highest.

| Result | What the unit does |
|---|---|
| Natural roll above its Resolve | **Breaks and flees** |
| Natural roll equal to or below its Resolve, modified roll above it | **Falls back in good order** |
| Modified roll equal to or below its Resolve, or a natural double 1 | **Gives ground** |

Most tests in the game pass or fail. The Break Test has three results: a line is first pushed,
then driven back, and only then broken.

Example: a unit with Rs 7 loses by 3. It rolls a 5. The natural 5 is not above 7, but 5 + 3 = 8
is, so it falls back in good order. Had it rolled 4, the modified 7 would let it simply give
ground.

### Gives ground

The unit stays in formation and steps back. It moves **2"** straight away from every enemy unit
engaging it, without turning, pivoting or changing its facing at all. The move must take it out
of base contact with the winners. It stops at once if it touches another unit or terrain, or if
going further would bring it within 1" of some other enemy unit.

Usually this means 2" straight back, or straight sideways, away from a single enemy. A unit
engaged in two neighbouring arcs, say its front and one flank, backs off diagonally, away from
both.

### Falls back in good order

The unit retreats in haste but keeps its spirit. Of the winning units, it moves straight away from
the one with the **highest Unit Mass** (chapter 2); if two tie, pick one of them at random. It moves just as a fleeing
unit does (chapter 4), except:

- For its flee roll it rolls **2D6 and keeps the higher** die (either one if they match).
- It **rallies automatically** at the end of the move.

**Overwhelmed.** If the winning side's Unit Mass is **more than twice** the losing side's, a
unit that rolled this result breaks and flees instead. Work out Unit Mass at the end of the
Melee Phase, not the start. Where a side has several units in the melee, add theirs together.

### Breaks and flees

The unit turns and runs. It flees straight away from whichever winning unit has the **highest
Unit Mass**, picking at random between two that tie. Fleeing moves are in chapter 4.

### Keeping apart

If a unit that broke or fell back is still touching an enemy unit after its move, push the units
apart by the least distance needed to keep them 1" apart. Agree together how to do it, so that
neither side gains from it.

### Surrounded

A losing unit may be unable to get out of contact with one or more of the units engaging it.
Then it stops moving at once, and the units stay locked where they are. They fight another round
in the next player's turn, just as after a draw.

## 2. Follow up and pursuit

After the Break Tests, but **before** any loser gives ground or rolls to flee, each winning unit
chooses what to do next. What it may choose depends on what its enemy did:

| The enemy… | The winner may… |
|---|---|
| Gives ground | Follow up, or restrain and reform |
| Falls back in good order or breaks | Pursue, or restrain and reform |
| Was wiped out before the Break Test | Overrun, or restrain and reform |

### Restrain and reform

Most units may try to hold their ground instead of chasing. The unit takes a **Restraint
test**, which is a Resolve Test.

- **Passed:** the unit holds its position and is allowed a free reform (chapter 4).
- **Failed:** the unit must follow up or pursue.

### Follow up

When an enemy unit it was fighting gives ground, a winning unit may follow it. Before moving, it
may **change facing** with a free turn of 90° or 180°. It then moves back into base contact with
the enemy. The two units are engaged again and stay locked until the next player's turn, when the
melee goes on.

### Pursuit

A winner may pursue **one** losing unit that fell back in good order or fled. If more than one
enemy unit lost, say which one you are chasing before anyone makes a flee roll.

Wait until every losing unit has made its move. Then make the pursuit moves one at a time, in the
order the winning player picks. For each:

1. Pivot the pursuer about its centre to face the unit it is chasing.
2. Roll 2D6: the **pursuit roll**.
3. Move the pursuer that many inches straight towards its quarry.

### Catching the quarry

If the pursuer reaches the unit it was chasing, it catches it and stops.

- **A fleeing unit** is cut down and removed, exactly as if a charging unit had run it down
  (chapter 4). The pursuer may then try to reform.
- **A unit that fell back in good order** is engaged again. The units stay locked until the next
  player's turn and fight on then; in that turn the pursuer counts as having charged.

### Running into something else

A pursuer often hits something other than its quarry.

- **Another fleeing enemy unit.** That unit is run down, just as if a charger had caught it. The
  pursuer may then try to reform.
- **A fresh enemy unit.** The pursuer counts as charging it, wheeling to bring as many models as
  possible into contact and aligning as for a normal charge. The unit it was chasing escapes.
- **An enemy unit that is not fleeing** becomes engaged with the pursuer:
  - If that unit was in a melee at the start of this phase, and that melee has not yet been
    fought, the pursuer joins it and fights again when it is fought, counting as having charged.
    It can't pursue a second time this turn: afterwards it restrains and reforms automatically,
    with no Restraint test.
  - Otherwise the units stay locked until the next player's turn and fight then, with the pursuer
    counting as having charged.
- **Terrain.** Pursuers are affected by terrain as normal. A pursuer stops as soon as it touches
  a friendly unit or impassable terrain.
- **The table edge.** If any part of a pursuer touches or passes the edge of the battlefield, take
  it off. It is not destroyed. In its owner's next Compulsory Moves step it comes back as
  reinforcements (chapter 4), placed as close as you can to where it left.

### Overrun

A unit that wipes out its enemy before the Break Test step may restrain and reform, or
**overrun**. An overrunning unit makes an ordinary pursuit move, but straight ahead, with no
pivot first.

### When a unit can't follow up or pursue

- **Still engaged.** A unit that is still touching an enemy unit may neither follow up nor
  pursue.
- **Blocked.** If friendly units or terrain stop a unit finishing the move it chose, it goes as
  far as it can and halts.

## 3. End of the turn

### No more foes

In a melee with several units, casualties can shrink units until some of them can no longer
reach the enemy. A unit that can't get back into base contact drops out of the melee at the end
of the turn.

### The turn ends

When every melee has been fought, the active player's turn is over and the other player's turn
begins. It is worth keeping count of the turns and rounds played so far.

## 4. Panic

Away from the melee, the sight of friends dying or running can shake a unit loose.

### Panic Tests

A **Panic Test** is a Resolve Test.

- **Passed:** the unit holds firm and nothing happens.
- **Failed:** the unit panics. How far it runs depends on what it has already lost:
  - Of the models it began the battle with, more than half (50%) are left: it **falls back in
    good order**.
  - Half (50%) or fewer are left: it **flees** at once.

The cause of the test says which way the unit runs. When two or more units of one army must test
at the same moment, their owner picks the order.

### Who doesn't test

A unit takes at most **one** Panic Test in each phase, however many causes come up. Most tests are
taken the moment they are caused; in a big game it helps to mark the units that have tested.

A unit never takes a Panic Test while it is:

- making a charge move,
- engaged in a melee, or
- fleeing and not yet rallied.

Rallying is in chapter 3.

### What causes Panic

Many special rules, spells and magic items call for a Panic Test, and each one explains when.
Four causes come up in almost every game.

| Cause | Who tests | A unit that fails flees from |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy casualties** | A unit that, in any one phase other than the Melee Phase, loses over a quarter (25%) of the models that were in it as that phase began | The enemy unit that caused the losses; if no enemy unit caused them, the nearest enemy unit that is not itself fleeing |
| **Nearby friend destroyed** | Every friendly unit within 6" of a destroyed unit of Unit Mass 5 or more | The nearest enemy unit that is not itself fleeing |
| **Nearby friend flees a melee** | Every friendly unit within 6" of a unit of Unit Mass 5 or more that lost a melee and then broke or fell back in good order | The nearest enemy unit that is not itself fleeing |
| **Fled through** | A unit that a friendly unit moves through while fleeing or falling back in good order | The nearest enemy unit that is not itself fleeing |

**Heavy casualties.** Losses in the Melee Phase never cause Panic Tests. In a melee, the Melee
Outcome and the Break Test do that job.

**Nearby friend destroyed, nearby friend flees.** Leave the unit that caused the tests where it
is until every test is taken, so there is something to measure from. Make all the tests, then
move the units that failed. It makes no difference whether the friend broke or only fell back in
good order: in the din of battle, nobody nearby can tell.

**Fled through.** Finish moving the fleeing unit first, then take the test. Beware the chain: one
unit flees through a second, which panics and flees through a third, and a whole battle line can
come apart.
