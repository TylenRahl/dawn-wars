# 10. War Machines

War machines are engines that throw heavy shot at the enemy: bolt throwers, cannons, stone
throwers and stranger devices. This chapter gives the rules and profiles for the common kinds.
Army lists add more unusual machines; most of them use these rules with a twist or an extra
special rule of their own. A war machine built on a profile from this chapter lists that weapon and
profile in its equipment.

Everything in chapter 5 still applies unless a rule below replaces it.

## War machines as a troop type

| Troop type | Unit Mass per model | Models per rank | Maximum rank bonus | Special rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| War machine | equal to its starting HP | - | - | Split Profile (war machine), Hired Crew, Weapon of War |

These special rules are explained with the war machine troop type in chapter 2.

## The machine and its crew

A war machine and its crew are **one model**. The crew are usually on bases of their own beside
the machine, so the machine's profile gives a base size for each. With separate crew bases you
can take one crew figure away each time the crew loses 1 HP, as a reminder.

- At deployment, set the crew figures touching the machine's base. They stay where they are,
  next to the machine, for the rest of the game.
- Ignore the crew figures when you measure or check line of sight.
- Mounting machine and crew together on one large base is equally fine.

A range written as two numbers is a minimum and a maximum (chapter 14). A Might or Pierce in
brackets is the value for the one model under the centre hole of the template (see Bombardment).

### Notes on each machine

- **Bolt throwers** shoot with the crew's Marksmanship and roll to hit as normal (chapter 5).
  Their Through & Through rule decides how many models a hit strikes.
- **Repeater bolt throwers** may fire the rapid fire profile instead of their normal shot, using
  the Rapid Fire rule.
- **Cannons and great cannons** never use the crew's Marksmanship. They fire with the Cannon Fire
  rule. A cannon of any size may fire **grapeshot** instead; grapeshot also ignores the crew's
  Marksmanship and uses the Scattershot rule. All of these use the **black powder misfire table**.
- **Fire throwers** never use the crew's Marksmanship. They shoot with the Column of Fire rule
  and use the **black powder misfire table**.
- **Volley guns** fire a hail of shots with the Multi-Barrelled rule and use the **black powder
  misfire table**. Their shots do roll to hit with the crew's Marksmanship.
- **Mortars** never use the crew's Marksmanship. They shoot as a stone thrower does, with the
  Bombardment rule, under a **5"** blast template, and use the **black powder misfire table**.
  Multiple Damage (D3) only applies to the single model under the template's centre hole.
- **Stone throwers** never use the crew's Marksmanship. They shoot with the Bombardment rule
  under a **3"** blast template and use the **stone thrower misfire table**. Multiple Damage
  (D3+1) only applies to the single model under the template's centre hole.

## How each machine shoots

Templates, the Artillery dice and the Scatter dice are described in chapter 1. A blast template
is a circle 3" or 5" across; a flame template is a teardrop about 8" long.

### Through & Through (bolt throwers)

A bolt can punch through rank after rank. When a bolt thrower aims at a formed unit of any
formation (Close Order, Open Order and the rest) and its roll to hit succeeds, it causes:

- bolt thrower in the target's **front or rear arc:** a hit on every rank, the rear rank
  included even if it is short;
- bolt thrower in the target's **flank arc:** a hit on every file.

Each hit after the first loses 1 Might. A hit whose Might drops to 0 does nothing, and no more
hits follow it.

### Rapid Fire (repeater bolt throwers)

Instead of its usual shot, a repeater bolt thrower may loose a spray of lighter bolts with the
rapid fire profile. All of those shots must go at one target.

### Cannon Fire (cannons)

1. **Pick a target point.** Choose any spot the cannon can see that is within its maximum range.
   It need not be an enemy model; open ground will do. Put a small marker on it.
2. **Fire.** Roll an Artillery dice.
   - **Misfire:** roll at once on the machine's misfire table.
   - **A number:** move the marker that many inches straight away from the cannon. Where it ends
     is the **strike point**, where the ball first hits the ground.
3. **Bounce.** If the cannon did not misfire, the ball bounces on from the strike point. Roll a
   second Artillery dice.
   - **Misfire:** the ball sticks in the ground and goes no further.
   - **A number:** the ball bounces that many inches, measured from the strike point, straight
     away from the cannon.
4. **Work out the hits.** Every model with its base under the strike point is hit automatically,
   and so is every model with its base under the ball's bounce path. When the bounce runs
   through a formed unit, it hits no more than:
   - one model in each rank, if the cannon is in that unit's front or rear arc;
   - one model in each file, if the cannon is in its flank arc.

   Models wholly past the end of the bounce are not hit.
5. **Stopped dead.** The ball stops at once, and hits nothing further, when:
   - it hits a monstrous creature or a behemoth, or
   - its path runs into a hill, a high linear obstacle or impassable terrain.

### Scattershot (grapeshot)

Packing a cannon with scrap is no exact science. Pick a target and check range in the usual way;
next, roll an Artillery dice.

- **Misfire:** roll at once on the machine's misfire table.
- **A number:** the target unit takes that many automatic hits.

### Column of Fire (fire throwers)

1. Lay the flame template with its narrow end touching the fire thrower's base anywhere along
   its front arc, and its broad end pointing at the target you want.
2. You may slide the template straight away from the fire thrower, as far as its maximum range,
   measured from the template's narrow end. The broad end may therefore reach past the maximum
   range.
3. Roll an Artillery dice.
   - **Misfire:** roll at once on the machine's misfire table.
   - **A number:** move the template that many inches straight away from the fire thrower. The
     flames come down where it stops.

Every model with its base under the template may be hit.

### Multi-Barrelled (volley guns)

Before rolling to hit, roll two Artillery dice. Add them together: that is how many shots the
volley gun fires.

- **A misfire on one dice:** the firing gear jams partway. Every shot this turn takes an extra
  -1 to hit.
- **A misfire on both dice:** nothing is fired. Roll at once on the machine's misfire table.

With the shot count known, roll to hit for every one with the crew's Marksmanship, applying
every modifier that fits.

### Bombardment (stone throwers and mortars)

1. **Pick a target.** Choose an enemy unit the machine can see that lies between its minimum and
   maximum range. Put the blast template with its centre hole right over the middle of that
   unit.
2. **Scatter.** Throw the Scatter dice and an Artillery dice together.
   - **Misfire on the Artillery dice:** roll at once on the machine's misfire table.
   - **Hit! on the Scatter dice:** the shot lands where it was aimed; ignore the Artillery dice.
   - **An arrow on the Scatter dice:** move the template as many inches as the Artillery dice
     shows, in the arrow's direction.
3. **Damage.** Every model with its base under the template may be hit.
   - One model whose base is under the template's centre hole takes the full force of the shot:
     its hit uses the higher Might and Pierce, the ones in brackets on the profile.
   - All other hits use the profile's normal Might and Pierce.

### Indirect fire

A machine that shoots with Bombardment may lob its shot high over troops and terrain at a target
it cannot see. Its crew are guessing where the enemy is from what scouts tell them, so the shot
is less accurate.

Say that you are firing indirectly before you shoot. The shot needs no line of sight to the
target unit; in every other way it is a normal shot, except for the scatter:

- **An arrow on the Scatter dice:** scatter as normal.
- **Hit! on the Scatter dice:** no direct hit this time. The shot still scatters, only less:
  move it in the direction of the small arrow printed above the Hit! symbol, by the Artillery
  dice score minus the crew's Marksmanship (to a minimum of 0").

## Misfire tables

When a machine misfires, roll a D6 on its table and apply the result straight away.

### Black powder misfire table

For every black powder weapon: cannons, volley guns, mortars, fire thrower and any stranger
device of the kind.

| D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Blown apart.** The weapon bursts. The model is destroyed and removed from play. |
| 2-4 | **Jammed.** A crewman is tangled in the works. The crew loses 1 HP at once. No shot this turn, and none again before the next round is over. |
| 5-6 | **Fizzle.** The fuse goes out. The model does not shoot this turn. |

### Stone thrower misfire table

| D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Blown apart.** The frame gives way under the strain. The model is destroyed and removed from play. |
| 2-4 | **Jammed.** A crewman is tangled in the works. The crew loses 1 HP at once. No shot this turn, and none again before the next round is over. |
| 5-6 | **Snap.** Something breaks, nothing serious. The model does not shoot this turn. |
