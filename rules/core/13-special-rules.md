# 13. Special Rules

The core rules cover what most warriors do. Anything a model does beyond them, from a beast that
breathes flame to a duellist who always strikes before the foe, is a **special rule**.

## How special rules work

### Three kinds

- **Universal special rules** can turn up in any army. They may belong to the model itself or
  to a piece of kit it carries. Every one of them is in this chapter.
- **Army special rules** belong to one army only. You find them listed with that army.
- **Unique special rules** belong to one model or one item. They are written out in full on its
  profile.

### Where a model gets them

A model's profile gives it most of its special rules, but it can pick up more along the way:

- from its **equipment**: usually weapons, but armour can carry rules too, and characters often
  gain them from magic items;
- from a **spell** cast on it;
- from some other **game effect**, such as the rules of a terrain feature it stands in.

Whatever grants the rule says so.

### Rules that add up

When a special rule changes a characteristic, a dice score or any other value (an armour value,
say), two copies of it **stack** unless the rule says otherwise. Add the effects together.

*Example:* a spell gives a model Armour Bane (2) and its sword has Armour Bane (1). The model now
has Armour Bane (3).

### When rules disagree

Rules sometimes pull in different directions. Settle it by this order, highest first:

1. **Unique** special rules beat everything below.
2. **Army** special rules beat universal rules and troop type rules.
3. **Universal** special rules and the rules of the model's troop type come last.

### Reading the brackets

Many rules carry a value in brackets after the name, written here as **X** (or **+X**, **-X**,
**X+**). The profile gives the real value, for example Fly (9) or Regeneration (5+). When that
value is a dice roll, the rule says when to roll it.

## The universal special rules

### Ambushers

Instead of deploying, you may keep this unit off the table in **reserve**.

1. From round two on, roll a D6 for every reserved Ambusher unit at the start of each of
   your turns (the Start of Turn sub-phase).
2. On **1–3** the unit waits; roll for it again next turn.
3. On **4+** it arrives as **reinforcements** in the Compulsory Moves sub-phase. You choose
   which table edge it comes on from, but it may not be set up within **8"** of any enemy model.
4. Any Ambushers still in reserve when round five begins arrive without a roll.

A reinforcement unit is placed with its rear arc flush against the table edge, facing the
middle of the table. In that turn it cannot march and counts as having moved when it shoots,
but otherwise it moves as normal in the Remaining Moves sub-phase.

### Armour Bane (X)

A **natural 6** on this model's roll to wound makes its weapon's **Pierce** X points better for
that wound.

*Example:* a weapon with no Pierce and Armour Bane (1) rolls a 6 to wound. Against that one wound
it counts as Pierce -1 when the target makes its Armour Roll.

### Armoured Hide (X)

The creature's own hide works as armour. It improves the armour value of the model, and of
anyone riding it, by X.

A model with no armour at all counts as having an armour value of **7+** when a rule improves it.

### Breath Weapon

Once per round, in its own Fire Phase, the model may breathe.

1. Lay the **flame template** with its wide end over the target and its narrow end touching the
   base of the breathing model, anywhere on its front arc.
2. The whole template must sit inside the model's vision arc.
3. Every model whose base is under the template may be hit.

The creature's own rules give the breath's Might and any special rules it has. A Breath Weapon
can't be used while the model is engaged in melee, or as part of a Stand & Shoot charge reaction.

### Chariot Runners

These light troops fight among the chariots.

- Friendly chariots can see over and through models with this rule.
- Friendly chariots can move through a friendly unit in Skirmish formation if most of its
  models have this rule. If a chariot would finish its move on top of a runner, push the runner
  aside by the least distance that makes room.
- While in Skirmish formation, a unit of Chariot Runners may count any friendly chariot within
  **1"** of at least one of its models as part of the unit when checking coherency.

### Close Order

A unit made of models with this rule may take up a **Close Order** formation (chapter 1).

### Counter Charge

Every model in the unit must have this rule for it to be used. When an enemy **cavalry, chariot
or monster** unit charges it in the front arc, it may answer with a **Counter Charge** charge
reaction:

1. Measure between the two units. If the gap is **less than the charger's Movement**, there is
   no time: the unit must Hold or Flee instead.
2. Otherwise, pivot the unit on its centre to face the centre of the charging unit.
3. Then move it **D3+1"** straight at the charger.
4. Both units count as having charged this turn.

A unit that is fleeing, or already in melee when charged, can't Counter Charge. It may Counter
Charge only one charger per turn. If several units charge it, wait until every charge is
declared, then the inactive player picks which one to meet; against the rest, the unit Holds.

### Cumbersome

A weapon with this rule can't be fired as part of a Stand & Shoot charge reaction.

### Detachment

A unit with this rule may be fielded as a **detachment** of a larger unit (see Regimental Unit,
and chapter 12).

### Dragged Along

If this model starts its move within **1"** of a friendly **infantry** unit of **ten or more**
models that is not fleeing, it may use that unit's Movement in place of its own.

### Drilled

- Just before it moves, a Drilled unit that is not fleeing gets a **redress the ranks**
  manoeuvre for free, then carries on with its move as normal.
- A Drilled unit may march within **8"** of the enemy without first taking a Resolve Test.

A character who joins a Drilled unit counts as Drilled too.

### Ethereal

- For movement, Ethereal models treat every kind of terrain as open ground. They may cross
  impassable terrain, but may not stop inside it.
- Only **Magical attacks** can wound an Ethereal model.
- An Ethereal character can't join a unit that isn't Ethereal, and a non-Ethereal character
  can't join one that is.

### Evasive

Once per turn, when the enemy names a unit as a target in its Fire Phase, and most of that
unit's models have this rule, the unit may **Fall Back in Good Order**, fleeing straight away
from the shooters. When the move is done, the enemy goes on with the shooting it declared.

### Extra Attacks (+X)

The model adds X to its Strikes. If X is a dice roll, roll it when the model's combat is picked
in a Choose & Fight Combat sub-phase.

### Fast Cavalry

A unit in **Open Order** in which every model, characters included, has this rule may make its
**Quick Turn** even after it marched.

### Fear

Models with this rule **cause Fear**. A Fear-causing enemy unit only counts if its **Unit
Mass is higher** than that of the unit facing it.

- **Charging a Fear-causer.** Before declaring the charge, the unit takes a **Resolve Test**.
  If it fails, it does not charge: it stays where it is and counts as having failed a charge. If
  it passes, it charges as normal.
- **Fighting a Fear-causer.** When the combat is picked in a Choose & Fight Combat sub-phase,
  each unit engaged with a Fear-causer takes a Resolve Test. If it fails, its models suffer
  **-1 to hit** with every attack aimed at the Fear-causing unit.

A unit takes at most **one** Fear test each turn. Models that cause Fear are not affected by
Fear. A unit that doesn't cause Fear gains no protection from a joined character that does.

### Feigned Flight

If this unit picks Flee (or Fire & Flee) as its charge reaction, it **rallies automatically**
at the end of that flee move.

### Fight in Extra Rank

This model may make **supporting attacks** (chapter 6).

### Fire & Flee

A unit armed with missile weapons, most of whose models have this rule, gains the **Fire &
Flee** charge reaction. It may use it if it can see the charging unit:

1. The unit Stands & Shoots at the charger as normal.
2. Then it flees. For its Flee roll it rolls **two D6 and drops the lower** (on a double, drop
   either), because the shooting cost it time.

If the charger is closer than its own Movement, there is no time to shoot: the unit must Hold or
Flee.

### First Charge

The first time in the game this unit charges and actually reaches its target, the target is
**Disrupted** until the end of that turn's Melee Phase.

### Flammable

This model may not take a Regeneration save against a wound from a **Flaming attack**.

### Flaming Attacks

Every attack made, and every hit caused, by this model, or by a weapon or spell with this rule,
is **Flaming**.

Unless told otherwise, the model's shooting and melee attacks are both Flaming. Its spells are
not, and nor are attacks made with any magic weapon it carries.

The model also **causes Fear** in **war beasts** and **swarms**.

### Fly (X)

Instead of walking, the model may fly, using its **Fly Movement** of X in place of its Movement.
It may not fly when it follows up or pursues. A flying model:

- moves just as it would on foot (charging, marching and manoeuvring), but passes over models,
  units and terrain freely;
- may march within **8"** of the enemy without a Resolve Test;
- may finish in terrain, and suffers that terrain's effects if it does;
- may not finish on top of impassable terrain or of another unit, nor closer than **1"** to the
  enemy.

Every move starts and finishes on the ground. A character that can Fly can't join a unit that
can't, and the other way round.

### Frenzy

A Frenzied model gets **+1 Strikes** whenever it charged this turn, and also in its next turn
after any follow up move. The bonus is for the model alone, never its mount (cavalry), the beasts
pulling it (chariot) or its rider (monster).

Also:

- If **most** of a unit's models are Frenzied, it passes every Fear, Panic and Terror test
  without rolling.
- If a unit with **any** Frenzied model can declare a charge in its Declare Charges & Charge
  Reactions sub-phase, it **must**.
- If **most** of a unit's models are Frenzied, it may never choose Flee as a charge reaction and
  may never choose to take a Restraint test.

**Losing Frenzy.** Frenzy, unlike other rules, can go away. A model that loses a round of melee
loses Frenzy at once.

### Furious Charge

The model gets **+1 Strikes** for the rest of any turn in which its charge move covered **3" or
more**.

### Hatred (X)

In the **first round** of a melee, the model may re-roll failed rolls to hit against an enemy
it hates. X names the hated foe. "All enemies" means it hates every enemy alike.

### Horde

The unit may claim a Rank Bonus **one higher** than the most its troop type normally allows.

### Howdah

A behemoth carrying a crewed platform. It has a **split profile** and uses both the Split
Profile rules for chariots and the Firing Platform rules (chapter 2). In every other way it is a
behemoth.

### Ignores Cover

When this model shoots, it ignores the to-hit penalties for partial and full cover.

### Immune to Psychology

If most of a unit's models have this rule:

- it passes every **Fear, Panic and Terror** test it has to take, without rolling;
- it may **not** choose Flee as a charge reaction.

It gives no help with any other test taken against Resolve.

### Impact Hits (X)

A model causes X **Impact Hits** (often a dice roll) if it charged, covered **3" or more** doing
so, and is touching the enemy.

They strike the charged unit as soon as its combat is picked (the first step of choosing and
fighting a combat), before anyone issues a challenge. Impact Hits **hit automatically** at the
model's **unmodified Might**.

### Impetuous

When a unit with **any** Impetuous model could declare a charge in its Declare Charges &
Charge Reactions sub-phase, it takes a Resolve Test. **Fail:** it must declare a charge.
**Pass:** you do as you like with it.

### Killing Blow

A **natural 6** on this model's roll to wound in melee is a **Killing Blow**, if the target's
troop type is **infantry or cavalry**:

- the target may take no Armour Roll and no Regeneration save against it (an Untouchable save is
  still allowed);
- if the wound goes unsaved, the target loses **all** its remaining Hit Points.

An attack that wounds automatically can't make a Killing Blow.

### Large Target

- Large Targets get no benefit from partial or full cover.
- Line of sight to a Large Target may be traced over or through another unit, unless that unit
  is a Large Target too. The same goes for a Large Target looking out.
- A unit shooting at a Large Target fires with **one extra rank**. Crossbows, for instance, fire
  with two ranks at a Large Target, or three from a hill.

### Levies

- Models with this rule can't use the General's **Inspiring Presence** or the Battle Standard's
  **Hold your Ground**.
- No one expects much of Levies. If a Levies unit of yours breaks and runs from a melee, your
  units without this rule need not take a Panic test for it.

### Loner

A Loner character can't be your General and can't join a unit that lacks this rule. A unit with
this rule can't be joined by a character who lacks it.

### Magic Resistance (-X)

Any enemy spell, Bound spells included, that targets a unit holding one or more models with
this rule takes **-X** on its Casting roll.

Magic Resistance does **not** stack. If several models in the unit have it, use the best one.

### Magical Attacks

Every attack made, and every hit caused, by this model, or by a weapon with this rule, is
**Magical**. All spells count as having this rule, and so do all hits caused by magic items.

### Mercenaries

An army may often take some units from another army's list as hired help. Each such unit gains
this rule.

- Mercenaries can't use the General's **Inspiring Presence** or the Battle Standard's **Hold
  your Ground**.
- No character from another army's list may join them.

### Monster Handlers

The monster comes with one or more **handler** models. At deployment, put them in base contact
with the monster, anywhere around it. If they get in the way of movement or line of sight, just
move them aside.

- In melee, each handler's attacks are added to the monster's.
- Roll a D6 for every unsaved wound the monster takes. On **1–4** the monster loses a Hit
  Point. On **5+** a handler is removed instead.
- Handlers leave play when their monster does.

### Monster Slayer

A **natural 6** on this model's roll to wound in melee is a **Monster Slaying Blow**, if the
target's troop type is **monster**:

- the target may take no Armour Roll and no Regeneration save against it (an Untouchable save is
  still allowed);
- if the wound goes unsaved, the target loses **all** its remaining Hit Points.

An attack that wounds automatically can't make a Monster Slaying Blow.

### Motley Crew

The unit may mix models of one type with different kit, or models of different types. Where
needed, the army list explains how the unit is made up.

- **Mixed weapons.** Roll the attacks of differently armed models in the fighting rank as
  separate batches. Tell your opponent which models each batch is for and where it is aimed.
  Each model still strikes at its own Reflexes step.
- **Mixed armour.** In melee, use the armour value most of the fighting rank has. Against
  shooting, use the one most of the unit has.
- **Casualties.** From shooting, share losses out as evenly as you can between the different
  models. In melee, take them from whichever models make up most of the fighting rank. Either
  way, the owner then moves models up from the rear ranks to fill the gaps.

### Move & Shoot

This weapon may fire in the Fire Phase even if its bearer marched this turn.

### Move or Shoot

This weapon may not fire in the Fire Phase if its bearer moved this turn for any reason, rallying
and reforming included.

### Move Through Cover

- Difficult and dangerous terrain never lowers this model's Movement.
- The model may re-roll any 1 on a Dangerous Terrain test.

### Multiple Damage (X)

Each **unsaved wound** from an attack with this rule costs the target **X Hit Points** instead of
one. Multiple Damage (2), for example, takes two. If X is a dice roll, roll it for each unsaved
wound separately.

Hit Points beyond what a model has left are wasted: they never pass on to other models in its
unit. The one exception is a character, where the excess counts towards **Overkill**.

### Multiple Shots (X)

The weapon may fire **once** as normal, or **X times** at **-1 to hit** on each shot. Every
model in the unit with this weapon must make the same choice. If X is a dice roll, roll it for
each model.

### Open Order

A unit made of models with this rule may take up an **Open Order** formation (chapter 1).

### Poisoned Attacks

A **natural 6** to hit from this model gives that hit **+2 on its roll to wound**. Unless told
otherwise, it works for both shooting and melee. It never works for the model's spells or for
attacks with magic weapons.

It can't be used by an attack that needs **7+** to hit, or that hits automatically.

### Ponderous

Moving and shooting costs this weapon **-2 to hit**, not the usual -1.

### Quick Shot

- The weapon suffers no -1 to hit for moving and shooting.
- A unit carrying it may Stand & Shoot however close the charger is.

### Rallying Cry

In the Command sub-phase of its turn, if it is not in melee, this character may pick **one**
fleeing friendly unit within its Command range. That unit takes a **Rally test** at once. If it
fails, it may try again as normal in the Rally sub-phase.

### Random Attacks

The model has no fixed Strikes; its profile gives a dice roll instead (D3+1, say). Each time it
attacks in melee, roll that to find how many attacks it makes, then roll to hit as normal. If
several such models are in a fighting rank, roll for each one unless told otherwise.

### Random Movement

The model has no fixed Movement; its profile gives a dice roll instead (2D6, say). Each time it
moves, roll that to find the most it may move.

- It moves in the **Compulsory Moves** sub-phase.
- It may not march or declare a charge.
- It may wheel to turn, but make no other manoeuvre.
- If its compulsory move or a pursuit can carry it into an enemy unit, it may go into contact,
  which counts as a charge. It lines up against that unit and stops. The unit it hits must
  **Hold**.

A unit in which all models have the rule makes one roll for the whole unit. If models in the unit have
different dice, roll each and the whole unit uses the lowest score.

### Regeneration (X+)

Straight after the model loses a Hit Point, before any model with none left is removed, it may
roll a D6 **Regeneration save**. On **X or more** it gets that Hit Point back. The lost point
still counts towards the Melee Outcome.

Rules that change armour values leave Regeneration values alone, unless they say otherwise.

### Regimental Unit

A unit with this rule may be accompanied by a **detachment** (see Detachment, and chapter 12).

### Requires Two Hands

In the Melee Phase, a model using this weapon can't use a shield as well. The shield still
works against wounds from shooting and magic in other phases.

### Reserve Move

At the end of its own Fire Phase, once all shooting is over, a unit most of whose models have
this rule may make a **Reserve move**. It can't if it charged, fled or marched earlier that
turn. It moves by the normal movement rules (chapter 4) and may manoeuvre, but may not march.

### Scouts

Scouts may deploy **after** every other unit of both armies, anywhere on the table more than
**12"** from any enemy model. Scouts deployed like this may not declare a charge in their first
turn.

If both armies have Scouts, roll off. The winner places a scouting unit first, then the players
take turns placing one unit each.

### Shieldwall

**Once per game**, if it was charged this turn, a **Close Order** unit with this rule that
carries shields, and chooses to use them, may **Give Ground** instead of Falling Back in Good
Order.

### Skirmishers

A unit made of models with this rule may take up a **Skirmish** formation (chapter 1).

### Stomp Attacks (X)

A model in base contact with the enemy makes X **Stomp Attacks**, often a dice roll. They come
**last** in the melee, after every other attack, even those made at Reflexes 1. They **hit
automatically** at the model's **unmodified Might**.

### Strike First

In the Melee Phase, while engaged, this model's Reflexes become **10** before any other
modifier is applied. A model with both Strike First and Strike Last has neither.

### Strike Last

In the Melee Phase, while engaged, this model's Reflexes become **1** before any other modifier
is applied. A model with both Strike Last and Strike First has neither.

### Stubborn

The **first** time this unit must take a Break Test, it may skip it and **Fall Back in Good
Order** instead. This works even when the winners' Unit Mass is over twice its own.

A joined Stubborn character does not make the unit Stubborn, and a Stubborn character can't use
the rule while in a unit that isn't.

### Stupidity

At the start of each of its turns (the Start of Turn sub-phase), a unit with this rule that is
neither fleeing nor in melee takes a **Resolve Test**. If it fails, it is **stupid** until the
start of its following turn. A stupid unit:

- may not move, except to flee;
- may not shoot or cast spells;
- may not try a Wizardly dispel;
- must Hold if charged.

Stupidity spreads: a unit or a mount without the rule gains it when a character with it joins
or rides it.

### Swiftstride

A unit made up wholly of models with this rule adds **3"** to its maximum charge range. Each time
it rolls to charge, flee or pursue, it may first opt to add **+D6** to the score.

### Terror

Models with this rule **cause Terror**, and cause Fear as well.

- **Charging.** When a Terror-causing unit declares a charge, its target takes a Resolve Test
  at once. On a fail it must **Flee**. On a pass it picks its charge reaction as normal. A unit
  that isn't allowed to Flee skips this test.
- **Breaking.** If one or more Terror-causing units are on the winning side of a melee, every
  unit on the losing side takes **-1 Resolve** for its Break Test.

Causing Fear gives no protection here: Fear-causing models still suffer Fear from Terror-causers.
Models that cause Terror are not affected by Terror. A unit that doesn't cause Terror gains no
protection from a joined character that does.

### Topple

*(A behemoth's fall.)* When this model drops to zero Hit Points, roll off. The winner chooses
which way it falls: into its front, flank or rear arc. Every unit in base contact with the model
inside that arc takes **D6 hits** at the model's Might with **Pierce -1**. After those hits, remove
the model.

### Unbreakable

A unit with this rule that loses a round of melee takes no Break Test. It **Gives Ground**
instead, pushed back by the foe. An Unbreakable character can't join a unit that isn't
Unbreakable, and the other way round.

### Unstable

When a unit with this rule loses a round of melee, it loses **one extra Hit Point for each
point** it lost the Melee Outcome by. Take them after the Melee Outcome is worked out and before
any Break Test. No Regeneration save can win them back.

If Unstable characters are in the unit, allocate these losses one to each model in turn until
every model has taken one, then split what is left as evenly as you can between the
character(s) and the unit.

### Vanguard

Once deployment is done, a unit with this rule may make a **Vanguard move**: it moves by the
normal movement rules (chapter 4) and may manoeuvre, but may not march.

If both armies have Vanguard units, roll off. The winner moves one first, then the players take
turns moving one each. A unit that made a Vanguard move may not declare a charge in its first
turn.

### Veteran

If most of a unit's models have this rule, it may re-roll any failed Resolve Test. A Break Test
is not a Resolve Test, so it can't be re-rolled this way.

### Volley Fire

When this unit shoots, its normal shooters (the front rank, or the front two on a hill) fire as
usual, and so do **half the models in each rank behind them**, rounding up. A rear-rank model
sees what the model at the front of its file sees.

A unit can't Volley Fire if it moved for any reason this turn, reforming included, or as a Stand
& Shoot charge reaction.

### Warband

- Unless it is fleeing, a Warband adds its current **Rank Bonus** to its Resolve, to no more than
  Resolve 10.
- It may not use that bonus for a Restraint test it chooses to take, or, if it is Impetuous, for
  the test to see whether it must charge.
- If most of the unit's models have this rule, its Charge roll may be re-rolled.

A character's Resolve gets the bonus only if the character is a Warband too. The unit uses the
highest of: its own boosted Resolve, a Warband character's boosted Resolve, or a non-Warband
character's plain Resolve.

### Weave-spawned

- This model may not take a Regeneration save against a wound from a **Magical attack**.
- A Weave-spawned character can't join a unit that isn't Weave-spawned, and the other way round.
