# 11. Terrain

This chapter covers setting up the battlefield and what each kind of terrain is. How units move
through terrain is in chapter 4; how terrain affects a fight is in chapter 6; cover and line of
sight for shooting are in chapter 5. Rules that exist only for terrain are given here in full.

## Setting up the battlefield

### How much terrain

Count one terrain feature for every 12" of the table's longest edge, rounding that edge up to
the next whole 12". A table whose longest edge is 72" gets six features.

### Size of a feature

A terrain feature measures from 2" to 12" across its widest point. Anything narrower than 2" is
not a terrain feature: it is **battlefield decoration** (below). A big feature uses up more of
your count:

| Widest point | Counts as |
|---|---|
| 2" to 8" | 1 feature |
| 8" to 12" | 2 features |
| Over 12" | 3 features |

### Placing terrain

A scenario may tell you which features to use and where they go. If it doesn't, set up like this:

1. Work out the number of features for your table (see How much terrain).
2. Each player picks features from the terrain you own, up to that number.
3. Roll off. The winner places one of the picked features.
4. Take turns placing one feature at a time, drawing from everything both players picked, until
   the agreed number is down. Put the rest away.

You may place a feature anywhere except within 12" of the centre of the table, and never within
12" of a feature your opponent placed. **Special features** are the exception: they go no more than 12"
from the centre.

If both players agree, or the scenario says so, battlefield decorations are set up the same way.

### Scattered placement

This is an optional replacement for Placing terrain, for a battlefield nobody chose.

1. Work out the number of features for your table.
2. Either pick features, up to that number, or roll them up on a terrain generator (end of
   this chapter).
3. Roll off. The winner places **all** the features wherever they like.
4. The loser then picks D3 of the placed features. For each, roll 2D6 and the scatter dice.
   On an arrow, the feature shifts that many inches the way the arrow points, halting if it
   meets another feature or the table edge. On a Hit!, it stays put.

No feature may be placed within 12" of the centre, except features the scenario names and
special features. Those never scatter once they are down.

Battlefield decorations may be set up the same way, if both players agree or the scenario says so.

## Categories of terrain

Every piece of terrain falls into one of seven categories: **open ground**, **woods**, **hills**,
**difficult terrain**, **dangerous terrain**, **impassable terrain**, and **linear obstacles**
(low or high).

### Open ground

Anything not given another category is open ground: fields, flats, dry earth and the like. It has
no effect on movement or fighting.

### Difficult terrain

Rough going: scrub, rocks, shallow water, fords, deep mud, and most woods.

- A unit any part of which starts in, crosses or ends in difficult terrain moves at **-1
  Movement** (never below 1) for that move.
- A unit charging through it rolls 2D6 for its charge as usual but drops the **higher** die,
  keeping the lower.
- If **at least a quarter (25%)** of a unit's models are in difficult terrain when it ends a move,
  or when a Melee Phase begins, the unit is **Disrupted** and gets no Rank Bonus.

Chapter 4 has the movement details and chapter 6 the effect in a fight.

### Dangerous terrain

Terrain that can hurt: broken rock, deep bog, fast rivers, unnatural ground, very dense woods,
steep stony hills.

Dangerous terrain slows units exactly as difficult terrain does. On top of that, every model,
alone or in a unit, that starts its move in it, crosses it or finishes in it takes a **Dangerous
Terrain test**: roll one die. A 2 or better means no harm. A **1** means the model loses 1 Hit Point.

A model tests once for each separate dangerous feature it meets in a single move. In a fight,
dangerous terrain counts as difficult terrain.

### Impassable terrain

Ground nothing can cross: rock spires, deep rivers, cliffs, most buildings, and high obstacles
such as castle walls. Units go round it. If impassable terrain stops a charging unit from lining
up with its target, the charge is **disordered**: the charger gets no Reflexes bonus for
charging (chapter 6).

### Combining categories

A feature can be more than one thing: a hill with a cliff face, a fence through a wood. Before
the game, agree how each part counts. For a hill with a sheer side, say, that side is impassable
and the rest is hill.

## Hills

A hill counts as **open ground**. Holding one helps in a fight (chapter 6) and when shooting.

**Seeing past a hill.** When a hill stands between two models and neither model is on it, they
can see each other only if a line from one base to the other clears the hill entirely, passing
neither over it nor through it.

**High ground.**

- A unit standing on a hill fires with **one more rank** than it could on the flat.
- A unit **wholly** on a hill sees across or through units and models that are not on a hill.
- The reverse also holds: a unit that is not on a hill sees across or through units and models
  to a unit wholly on a hill.

## Woods

A wood can be difficult, dangerous or even impassable, depending on how big and thick it is, and
it always blocks sight to some degree. In a fight, woods count as difficult terrain (chapter 6).

**Edge.** A wood needs a clear boundary, so you can lift out single trees to fit models in. What
matters is the boundary, not where the trees stand: a model whose body is mostly (over half)
inside the edge is in **partial cover**, and so is a unit with over half its models inside.

**Seeing past a wood.** When a wood stands between two models and neither is inside it, they can
see each other only if a line from base to base runs neither over nor through the wood.

## Linear obstacles

Walls, hedges, fences, ditches and furrows. Under **2"** tall is a **low** linear obstacle;
**2" or taller** is a **high** one.

### Low linear obstacles

- For movement, a low obstacle is **difficult terrain**.
- A unit in a fight that ends up straddling one is **Disrupted** and gets no Rank Bonus.

### High linear obstacles

A high obstacle, a castle wall for example, is **impassable terrain** for both movement and
fighting.

### Defending a low obstacle

A unit standing behind a low obstacle defends it by bringing its front rank into base contact
with it. The enemy may charge it as normal without climbing over: the charger's front rank
comes into base contact with the far side of the obstacle instead. Any unit charging defenders
behind a low obstacle makes a **disordered** charge, unless it has **Fly (X)**.

## Linear terrain features

Rivers, streams, chasms, ravines and roads: features cut into the ground rather than standing up
from it.

### Sections

A linear feature is built from **sections**, each **12" long**. A **narrow** section is up to 4"
wide; a **wide** one is over 4" and up to 8". Each section counts as one terrain feature for How
much terrain. A section may hold one bend of no more than 90°, or one fork or junction.

How long the whole feature may be depends on what it is:

- **Hazardous** (counts as dangerous or impassable, such as a fast river or a deep chasm): no
  more than half the table's longest edge. On a 48" x 72" table that is 36".
- **Easy** (counts as open ground or difficult, such as a small stream or a good road): any
  length, winding round other features as you like.

### Placing linear features

Place them with the rest of the terrain. One end must touch a table edge. The other end must do
one of these:

- touch a different table edge;
- stop where it meets another feature (a stream running into a pond, say);
- narrow to a point and stop.

### Bridges and fords

Bridges can cross streams, rivers, chasms, ravines and gullies; fords can cross rivers and
streams. Each 12" of linear feature may have **one** bridge or ford up to **3"** wide. On longer
features you may join them into wider crossings. A bridge is **open ground**; a ford is
**difficult terrain**.

### Fast-flowing rivers

If a river runs from one table edge to another, you may make it fast-flowing. If so, randomly decide which way it
runs. At the Start of Turn sub-phase, any unit that has at least a quarter (25%) of its models in the
river takes a **Might test**. If it fails, the unit is carried **D3"** downstream.

### Good roads

When a unit marches, and **at least half (50%)** of its models are on one well-maintained road
both at the start and at the end of the march, and it stays on that road throughout, it may go **D3"** further.

## Buildings

Buildings are normally **impassable terrain**. If both players agree, a building at least **4"**
across its widest point can be made **occupiable**.

### Occupying a building

- Only an **infantry** unit with a **Unit Mass no higher than 20** may occupy one.
- To go in, the unit moves into base contact with the building in the Remaining Moves sub-phase
  of its Manoeuvre Phase. When its move is over, take it off the table and note which building
  it holds.
- Measure all distances to and from the unit from the building itself.
- The unit sees all round (a 360° vision arc) and can see over or through other models.
- The unit may not declare a charge while inside.

### Leaving

A unit that is not fleeing may come out in the Remaining Moves sub-phase of its Manoeuvre Phase,
or as part of a follow up or pursuit. Set it on the table with at least one model touching the
building. Its move is then over.

### Fleeing inside

A unit that flees while in a building stays where it is. It counts as fleeing and must rally as
usual; it simply hides behind the walls instead of running.

### Shooting from and at a building

- **Out:** no more than **half (50%)** of the models inside may shoot or cast spells.
- **In:** anyone who can see the building may target the unit inside, which counts as being in
  **full cover**.
- **Templates:** if a template touches an occupied building, every model inside may be hit, but
  each is hit only on a D6 roll of **5+**, not the usual 4+.
- **Cannon:** a cannonball that hits an occupied building stops dead. The unit inside takes **D3
  hits at Might 4, Pierce -1**.

### Fighting at a building

- A charge at the unit inside is made against the building, not the models.
- While the fight lasts, both units count as being in **Skirmish** formation, whatever their real
  formation, and no more than **five** models from each side may be in the fighting rank.
- The unit inside never Gives Ground or Falls Back in Good Order. It stays and the fight goes on.
- If the unit inside Breaks, it cannot get out: it is destroyed and removed. A unit from the
  winning side may then take over the building.

## Battlefield decoration

Decorations are pieces under 2" across: a pile of crates, a wayside shrine. You place them by the
terrain method, using the same count, and a scenario may call for some as objectives.

Decorations are **ignored** for movement and fighting, as though they were not on the table. If
one gets in the way of placing a unit, mark where it stood, lift it out, and put it back when
convenient. They **can** block line of sight.

## Special features

A special feature is a notable landmark. A scenario may name one; otherwise the players may
agree to use one.

- Use **one** special feature only, unless the scenario says otherwise.
- It obeys all the normal rules for the kind of terrain it is, plus its own rules.
- Its position must be **no more than 12" from the table's centre**.

### Control

A unit controls a special feature, and through it so does that unit's army. Each feature says
which of two ways it is controlled:

**By proximity.** Any unit within **6"** of the feature, whose **Unit Mass is 5 or more**,
can control it, provided it is not fleeing. If more than one such unit, from opposing sides, is within
6", the closest one controls it. If two opposing units are equally close, the feature is
**contested** and no one controls it.

**By occupancy.** Only a unit inside the feature controls it. Only **one** unit fits in a
special feature.

- To go in, the unit moves into base contact with it in the Remaining Moves sub-phase of its
  Manoeuvre Phase. When its move is over, place it inside.
- While inside it may not charge. It may shoot and cast spells, seeing all round (a 360° arc)
  from the feature.
- Any unit or Wizard that can see the feature may shoot at it or target it with spells, but the
  unit inside counts as in **full cover**.
- Measure to and from the unit from the base of the feature, not from its models.
- A unit that is not fleeing may come out in the Remaining Moves sub-phase of its Manoeuvre
  Phase, or as a follow up or pursuit. Set it up in formation with at least one model touching
  the feature. Its move is then over.

**Benefits** of control often reach other units in the same army as well. They last only while
the feature stays controlled.

### Weave Monolith

A standing stone where magic gathers. Controlled **by proximity**.

- The unit controlling it gains **Magic Resistance (-1)**.
- If the controller is a Wizard, it may re-roll one die that comes up a natural 1 on any Casting
  roll.
- **Victory Points:** at the battle's end, whoever holds control of it scores **50** extra.

### Dark Ruins

Tumbled walls with an uneasy air. They are a building, so impassable terrain. They can be
occupied by an **infantry** unit with a **Unit Mass no higher than 20**, or by any single
character, whatever its troop type. Controlled **by occupancy**.

- **Dread.** If the unit inside causes Fear or Terror, enemy units take **-1 Resolve** on any
  Resolve Test caused by that Fear or Terror. If the unit inside causes neither, it becomes
  immune to both.
- **Strongpoint.** A charge at the unit inside is made against the ruins, not the models. Every
  arc of the unit inside counts as its front arc, so the charger gets no flank or rear bonus.
  The unit inside always counts as behind a defended low linear obstacle.
- **Holed up.** A unit that flees while inside does not move. It counts as fleeing and must
  rally as usual. In a fight it never Gives Ground or Falls Back in Good Order; it stays and the
  fight goes on. If it Breaks, it is destroyed and removed.
- **Templates:** if a template hits the ruins, every model inside counts as under it, but each is
  hit only on a **5+**, not 4+.
- **Cannon:** a cannonball that hits the occupied ruins stops dead, and the unit inside takes
  **D3 hits at Might 4, Pierce -1**.
- **Victory Points:** at the battle's end, whoever holds control of it scores **100** extra.

### Monument of Glory

A statue, cairn or column raised to past victories. Controlled **by proximity**.

- The unit controlling it may re-roll failed Panic tests, and every unit in that army may
  re-roll failed Rally tests.
- The unit controlling it gains **Hatred (all enemies)**.
- **Victory Points:** at the battle's end, whoever holds control of it scores **50** extra.

### Tower

A tall lone tower. It is a building, so impassable terrain. It can be occupied by an
**infantry** unit with a **Unit Mass no higher than 10**, or by a lone **infantry** character.
Controlled **by occupancy**.

- **Hard to reach.** Only enemy infantry units, infantry characters, or units with **Fly (X)**
  may charge the unit inside.
- **Fighting.** A charge at the unit inside is made against the Tower, not the models. While the
  fight lasts, both units count as being in **Skirmish** formation whatever their real
  formation, and no more than **five** models from each side may be in the fighting rank.
- **Lookout.** The unit inside sees across and through other units and models, and other units
  likewise see across and through units and models to it.
- **Holed up.** A unit that flees while inside does not move. It counts as fleeing and must
  rally as usual. In a fight it never Gives Ground or Falls Back in Good Order; it stays and the
  fight goes on. If it Breaks, it is destroyed and removed.
- **Templates:** if a template hits the Tower, every model inside counts as under it, but each is
  hit only on a **5+**, not 4+.
- **Cannon:** a cannonball that hits the occupied Tower stops dead, and the unit inside takes
  **D3 hits at Might 4, Pierce -1**.
- **Victory Points:** at the battle's end, whoever holds control of it scores **100** extra.

## Terrain generators

A terrain generator rolls up your features at random, so nobody's taste decides the table. Roll
2D6 on a table for each feature you need.

### Sizes from a generator

- Each rolled feature is 2" to 8" across its widest point.
- If the same result comes up again, either add a second feature of that type and size, or swap
  the first for a bigger one, 8" to 12" across.
- A repeated **linear** feature instead adds its length to the first.
- A repeated **building** result lets you either add more buildings, each up to 6" on its longest
  side, or swap an existing one for a single building 6" to 12" on its longest side.

### Bleak highlands

| 2D6 | Feature |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Road.** Linear, narrow or wide. A wide road is well-maintained (see Good roads). A narrow road is badly kept: each 12" section must contain a 3" stretch of difficult terrain. |
| 3 | **Dense woods.** Dangerous terrain, but the Dangerous Terrain test is failed on a **1-2**, not just a 1. |
| 4 | **Hill.** May have one stony slope, which counts as difficult or dangerous terrain. |
| 5 | **Ravine.** Linear, narrow or wide. Narrow: difficult terrain. Wide: dangerous terrain. |
| 6 | **Standing stones.** Difficult terrain. No line of sight can be drawn across it. |
| 7 | **Scrubland.** A kind of wood; difficult or dangerous terrain. |
| 8 | **Rocky ground.** Difficult or dangerous terrain. |
| 9 | **Haunted ruins.** One building up to 6" on its longest side, plus an enclosure no more than 12" on its longest side. A low linear obstacle runs along at least two edges of the enclosure. |
| 10 | **Lava cracks.** Dangerous terrain, but the Dangerous Terrain test is failed on a **1-3**. |
| 11 | **Chasm.** Linear, wide and impassable. Each 12" section must have a bridge. |
| 12 | **Bottomless pit.** Impassable, 3" to 6" across its widest point. |
