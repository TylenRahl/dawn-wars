# 4. Movement and Charges

The Manoeuvre Phase is where you place your army: you close on the enemy, guard your flanks,
line up your shooters and, when the moment comes, charge. A charge is the **only** way a unit
moves itself into melee. You cannot simply walk into an enemy unit; you must declare a charge
first.

The rules in this chapter are written for units in **Close Order**. Units in other formations
move differently; their rules are in chapter 2.

## The 1" rule

One rule holds for every move in the game: **no unit may finish a move within 1" of an enemy
unit**, unless it is engaged in melee with it.

A unit may pass within 1" of an enemy while it moves, as long as there is at least 1" between
them once the move is over. If two units end up too close, the players nudge them apart by the
smallest distance that restores the gap. Agree together how to do it, and do it so that neither
side gains from it.

## The phase in four steps

The Manoeuvre Phase has four steps. Finish each step before starting the next. Within a step,
your units act in any order you like.

1. **Declare charges and charge reactions.** You name your charging units one at a time, each
   with the enemy unit it will charge. When all charges are named, your opponent declares and
   resolves a reaction for every unit that was charged.
2. **Charge moves.** You move your charging units, one at a time, in the order you choose.
   Finish each charge before starting the next.
3. **Compulsory moves.** Units that have no choice about moving (fleeing units, for one) move
   now.
4. **Remaining moves.** You move the rest of your army: every unit that did not charge and did
   not move in step 3.

---

## Step 1: Declare charges

At the very start of the phase, say which of your units (if any) will charge, and which enemy
unit each will charge. The enemy unit is the **charge target**. No unit has to charge unless a
special rule makes it.

To declare a charge:

- the target must sit at least partly inside your unit's **front arc**, and
- at least one of your models must be able to see it.

You may always measure the distance to a possible target before you decide. Look out for
terrain on the way; it can slow the charge (see Charging through terrain, below).

### Who cannot charge

These units can neither declare a charge nor carry one out:

- already engaged in melee,
- fleeing, or
- a unit that rallied in this turn's Strategy Phase.

A unit in **Marching Column** may declare a charge, but cannot make the charge move. Some special
rules and spells also stop a unit from declaring a charge or from making the charge move.

### Impossible charges

You may not declare a charge that cannot succeed: one where the target is beyond the unit's
maximum possible charge range, or where something in the way leaves no route into contact.

A unit's **maximum possible charge range** is its current Movement, with every modifier to
Movement applied, plus 6 (the best an unmodified Charge roll can give).

If the obstacle is a unit that might get out of the way before the charge is made, the charge counts as possible and may be declared. You still need line of
sight to the target.

### Charging more than one unit

As a rule, one charge has one target. If it cannot reach its chosen target without touching
one or more other enemy units, it must declare a charge against each of them too. Every unit it
will touch is then a charge target and makes its own charge reaction. The player who owns them
picks the order in which they react.

## Step 1 (continued): Charge reactions

When you have declared all your charges, your opponent picks a reaction for each charge
target. There are three: **Hold**, **Stand and Shoot** and **Flee**. A unit whose player forgets
to pick one Holds.

| Charged unit is... | Hold | Stand and Shoot | Flee |
|---|---|---|---|
| free (not fleeing, not in melee) | yes | yes, if it qualifies | yes |
| already engaged in melee | must | no | no |
| already fleeing | no | no | must |

### Hold

The unit stays where it is and meets the charge. This is the usual choice for a unit with no
missile weapons, or one that likes its odds in the fight.

### Stand and Shoot

A unit with missile weapons that can see the charging unit may choose to Stand and Shoot.

1. Measure the distance between the two units.
2. If it is **less than the charger's Movement**, the charger is too close: the unit cannot
   shoot and must Hold or Flee instead.
3. Otherwise the unit shoots at the charger (chapter 5), **even if the charger is beyond the
   weapons' maximum range**.
4. Then the unit Holds.

A charging unit that takes casualties from Stand and Shoot never takes a Panic test for them.

**More than one charger.** A unit may Stand and Shoot against only one charge each turn. If
several units charge it, its player waits until all charges are declared, picks which charger
it shoots at, and the unit Holds against the others.

### Flee

Any unit not engaged in melee may Flee from a charge. The unit turns to face directly away from
the charger and runs:

1. Spin it about its centre so its back points squarely at the middle of the charger.
2. Make a flee move straight away (see Fleeing, below).

If it does not get far enough, the charger may catch it and destroy it (see Charging a fleeing
enemy, below).

If several units charged it, it flees directly away from the one with the highest Unit
Mass (chapter 2). If two are tied, pick one at random. In its rush it may run straight
through another of the chargers.

## Step 2: Charge moves

With every reaction resolved, you find out which charges arrive. Take your charging units one
at a time, in any order you choose, and finish each charge before the next.

### Charge range

A charge is faster than a normal move, and less certain. For each charging unit:

1. Make a **Charge roll**: roll 2D6 and keep the higher die. If both dice show the same number,
   keep either one.
2. Its **charge range** in inches is its Movement plus that roll.
3. Make the charge move. If the charge range is too short to reach the target, the charge
   **fails** (see Failed charge).

### Charging through terrain

A charging unit must take the shortest route to its target, so it will often have to cross
difficult or dangerous terrain, or a low linear obstacle.

Before you roll, look at the route. If any part of the unit will cross difficult or dangerous
terrain, or a low linear obstacle, during the charge move:

- roll the 2D6 as usual but keep the **lower** die, not the higher, and
- the unit's Movement is reduced by 1 for the charge move (to no less than 1).

When the charge move ends with at least a quarter (25%) of the unit's models in
difficult terrain, or sit astride a low linear obstacle, the unit is **Disrupted** and cannot claim
a Rank Bonus.

### How a charger moves

A charging unit has four duties:

1. Bring as many models of its front rank as it can into base contact with the charge target.
2. Take the shortest route to the charge target.
3. Move forward as straight as it can.
4. When it has moved, end up aligned against the charge target.

**Manoeuvring.** Unless a rule says otherwise, the only manoeuvre open to a charger is the
wheel: no turning, reforming, redressing, and no stepping back or to the side. It may **wheel once**, at any point in the move, to
bring as many models as possible from both sides into contact. It need not finish that wheel
if finishing it would make its own charge fail, or would stop another unit's declared charge
from being completed.

After any wheel, the unit moves straight on towards the target and stops the moment the two
units touch.

### Aligning to the enemy

A charger usually touches its target at an angle, corner to edge. Close the gap: once contact
is made, the charger makes a second wheel, which is **free**, so that its front sits flush
against the side of the target it charged.

### Unable to align

Sometimes a charger reaches its target but cannot align, because something is in the way. Then
the charge target aligns itself against the charger instead. If that is not possible either,
leave the gap. Either way the charger has made a **disordered charge**.

### Disordered charge

A unit that made a disordered charge does not get the Reflexes bonus for charging (chapter 6).

### Multiple charge targets

When a charge has more than one target (see Charging more than one unit), each target reacts
on its own. If, after contact, the charger cannot align to all of them, every target it cannot
align to must align itself against the charger instead. Against each target that had to align,
the charger has made a disordered charge.

### Multiple chargers

When two or more units charge into the **same arc** of one enemy unit, move them at the same
time. Together they must bring as many models of both sides into base contact as they can.

### Front, flank and rear charges

Hitting an enemy from the side or behind gives you the upper hand in melee (chapter 6). Which
face of the target you charge is fixed by where the charger stands **when the charge is
declared**, not by where it ends up:

| Charger is in the target's... | It charges the target's... |
|---|---|
| front arc | front |
| flank arc | flank |
| rear arc | rear |

Units usually start a battle facing one another, so front charges are the most common.

**Straddling two arcs.** If the charger lies across two arcs, count the models of its front
rank in each. It is in the arc that holds more of them. If that still does not settle it, the
charger is in whichever arc helps it least in the Melee Outcome: between flank and rear, for
example, it is in the flank.

### Failed charge

A unit whose charge range falls short starts off but runs out of steam. It moves straight
towards the target by a distance equal to its **Charge roll** (not its charge range), wheeling
as needed. It does not make contact.

### Unclear cases

Odd situations will turn up when units charge. If one cannot be settled quickly from the rules,
the players agree on the most sensible outcome and move on, rather than argue at length.

### Charging a fleeing enemy

If the charge target Flees, wait until its flee move is done. The charger then either tries to
run it down or tries to redirect its charge.

**Running down.** The charger makes its charge move as normal.

- If it reaches the fleeing unit, it wheels to align as usual, and the fleeing unit is cut down
  and removed from play. The charger may then take a Resolve Test: if it passes, it may make a
  free reform; if it fails, it may not reform. Either way it cannot move again this turn.
- If it does not reach the fleeing unit, it moves its full charge range straight on. This is
  not a failed charge; its target simply got away.

Chapter 7 has more on catching fleeing units in melee and pursuit.

**Redirecting.** The charger may switch to a new target if there is a valid one. A valid new
target is:

- any unit that was a legal charge target for it when charges were declared this phase, or
- a unit that the fleeing unit's move has uncovered.

To redirect, the charger takes a Resolve Test.

- **Failed:** it must try to run down the fleeing unit.
- **Passed:** it may redirect, and declares a charge against the new target at once.

The new target has no time to Stand and Shoot; it may only Hold or Flee. If it Flees, the
charge cannot be redirected a second time. When a charge is redirected, the first target is not
run down and escapes. If there is no valid new target, the charge cannot be redirected.

### Accidental contact during a charge

A charging unit may find another enemy unit in its path, most often one that fled from a
different charge. If the charger can wheel around it and still complete its charge, it must.
If it cannot, or if doing so would make the charge fail, the charger either **halts** or
**continues ahead**.

**Halting.** The charger moves towards its target as usual and stops 1" short of the unit
blocking it. A halted charge does not count as a failed charge.

**Continuing ahead.** What happens depends on the unit in the way:

- **A fleeing unit:** the charger wheels to align as usual. The fleeing unit is run down and
  removed from play. The charger's move ends there, and in the confusion it may not try to
  reform.
- **A unit that is not fleeing:** treat the charge as redirected against that unit.

### Accidental contact while running down

A charger trying to run down a fleeing target may touch a different enemy unit on the way. If
so, the charge counts as redirected against that unit, and the fleeing unit gets away. If it
touches two or more enemy units, treat them as multiple charge targets (above).

### Accidental contact outside a charge

Sometimes, often as units shift during melee, a unit ends up touching an enemy unit it did not
charge. If this cannot be avoided:

- **Touched in its front arc, and not already in melee:** the touched unit's player chooses one:
  - fight: it aligns against the unit that touched it. This is not a charge for either side,
    and the melee is fought in Reflexes order; or
  - Give Ground (chapter 7) to stay out of a fight it does not want.
- **Touched in its flank or rear arc, or already in melee:** its player moves it aside, together
  with any units it is fighting if needed, by the least distance that leaves it 1" from the unit
  that touched it while still engaged in its own melee.

## Step 3: Compulsory moves

Most of the time you move your units as you please. Some units, though, move on their own.
Every compulsory move happens in this step, after charges and before any other movement.

1. **Fleeing units move first.** Any unit that failed to rally in the Strategy Phase
   (chapter 3) keeps fleeing now, before any other compulsory move (see Fleeing, below).
2. **Then all other compulsory moves**, in any order the owning player likes. These follow the
   normal movement rules unless their own rules say otherwise: a unit might have a random
   Movement, or be made to go in a set direction or a random one.

### Reinforcements

A unit arriving as reinforcements comes on in this step. Place it with its rear arc wholly
against a battlefield edge, facing the centre of the table. The rule that brings it on says
which edge, and where.

In the Remaining Moves step it may move normally, except that it **cannot march**. It counts as
having moved for shooting.

---

## Fleeing

A unit flees for many reasons: it failed to rally, it ran from a charge, it broke in melee
(chapter 7), and so on. A fleeing unit drops all formation and runs for safety by the most
direct way.

### Direction of flight

Whatever made the unit flee sets its direction, and usually that is an enemy unit.

- **Fleeing from an enemy unit:** pivot the unit about its centre until it faces directly away
  from the centre of that enemy unit, then make the flee move.
- **Fleeing from two or more enemy units at once:** the biggest threat wins: run from the one
  with the highest Unit Mass. Break a tie at random.
- **Fleeing as a compulsory move:** a unit that failed to rally keeps to its earlier path. It
  does not pivot; it moves straight ahead the way it is facing.

### Pivoting

To pivot, spin the unit on the spot about its centre. A pivot is not one of the manoeuvres
below, and it ignores other units and terrain while it is made.

### The flee move

After any pivot, make a **Flee roll**: roll 2D6. The total is how many inches the unit moves. A
fleeing unit:

- runs straight forward, the way it now faces,
- must move the **full** distance rolled, even off the battlefield, and
- may change direction only by pivoting, and only to get around impassable terrain.

**Once per phase.** Even a panicked unit has limits: one flee move per phase at most. Any
further order to flee in that same phase is carried out with no pivot and a distance of 0".

### Fleeing through friendly units

A fleeing unit has no formation to keep, so it passes through friendly units without slowing.
If its move would end on top of a friendly unit, it keeps going straight on until it is clear,
then stops.

### Fleeing through enemy units

Running through the enemy is deadly. When the flee move is over, roll a D6 for each model that
passed through an enemy unit: this is a **Peril test**.

| D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | The model is struck and loses 1 Hit Point. |
| 4+ | The model gets through unharmed. |

A single move can call for any number of Peril tests.

If its move would end on top of an enemy unit, or within 1" of one, the fleeing unit keeps
going straight on until it is 1" past that unit, then stops.

### Fleeing through terrain

A fleeing unit ignores the Movement penalty for difficult and dangerous terrain, but it still
takes any Dangerous Terrain tests. If it runs into impassable terrain, it pivots about its
centre and goes around it by the shortest route.

### Off the table

Should a fleeing unit touch or cross, with any part of it, the edge of the battlefield, the **whole unit
is removed** and counts as destroyed.

### Retreats that keep formation

Two retreats stop short of full flight: Giving Ground, and Falling Back in Good Order. They follow a lost
round of melee or heavy losses to shooting; chapter 7 says when each one happens. How they move:

- **Give Ground:** the unit keeps its formation and backs off 2", straight away from whichever
  enemy forced it to. It does not turn, pivot or change its facing. It halts at once on touching
  another unit or terrain, or if going further would break the 1" rule against an enemy.
- **Falling back in good order:** the unit is moved just like a fleeing one, with two
  differences. Its Flee roll is 2D6 keeping the higher die (either one if they match), and it
  rallies automatically at the end of its move.

## Step 4: Remaining moves

With charges and compulsory moves done, move the rest of your army. Now you set up next turn's
charges, keep the enemy from making theirs, put your shooters and Wizards where they have
targets, and take hold of key ground.

Fleeing units, units that **charged** this turn, and units that moved in the **Compulsory Moves**
step have all used their movement for the turn and does not move again now.

Your Wizards may cast **Conveyance** spells at any point in this step (chapter 9). A Conveyance
spell that needs a target may only target a friendly unit, and each Conveyance spell can work on
a given unit only once per turn.

### Basic movement

A unit moves straight forward up to its Movement in inches. If its models have different
Movement values, the whole unit moves at the speed of the slowest.

Ranked units are clumsy and slow to change direction, so a unit moves only in a straight line
forwards unless it makes a manoeuvre.

### Lone models

A single model, such as a character, a monster or a chariot, is a unit, and moves by the same
rules as any other unit.

### Manoeuvres

The table below lists the six manoeuvres. A unit may make **one** manoeuvre in its move. Whatever it does, no
model may move more than **twice its Movement**.

| Manoeuvre | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel | Swings the unit round a front corner as it advances | Distance moved by the outside model |
| Turn | Faces every model to the side or rear | A quarter of Movement per 90° |
| Move backwards | Moves the unit straight back | Movement is halved |
| Move sideways | Moves the unit straight to one side | Movement is halved |
| Redress the ranks | Puts up to five models into, or takes up to five out of, the front rank | Half of Movement |
| Reform | Pivots up to 180° and rearranges the ranks and files | All of the unit's movement |

#### Wheel

Wheeling lets a unit change the way it faces and still go forward. The front edge of the unit
swings forward while one front corner stays put as the pivot.

The whole unit is treated as having covered the distance travelled by its outermost model. When the wheel is done the unit may use whatever movement it has left. Unless it is
charging, a unit may wheel more than once in its move, switching between going forward and
wheeling as often as it likes.

#### Turn

Each model stays where it is and turns 90° or 180° to face the unit's side or rear. Each 90°
costs a quarter of the unit's Movement.

Models in full ranks just turn in place. Models in an incomplete rear rank move to the back of
the new formation. Command models (chapter 8) go straight into the new front rank; if it has no
room for them, they go into the second rank.

#### Move backwards

A unit may step backwards, but carefully: when it does, its Movement is halved.

#### Move sideways

A unit may sidestep, but slowly, to keep its ranks: when it does, its Movement is halved.

#### Redress the ranks

A unit spends half its Movement to take up to five models out of its front rank, or to put up
to five more into it, moving them to or from the ranks behind. Then rearrange the other ranks to
match the new front rank. Every rank must hold the same number of models; only the last rank
may be short.

#### Reform

A unit gives up **all** of its movement. In return it may spin about its centre to any facing
up to 180° from where it was, and lay out its ranks and files afresh: a new shape, or a different
formation altogether.

### Marching

Most units can **march** to cross the battlefield fast. A marching unit doubles its Movement.
It may wheel to change direction, but may make no other manoeuvre. A unit in Marching Column in
Close Order triples its Movement when it marches instead.

A unit that marched may not shoot in the Fire Phase that turn, and that includes casting Magic
Missile and Magical Vortex spells.

#### Enemy sighted

Troops are slow to march with the enemy close. To start a march while within 8" of any enemy
unit that is not fleeing, the unit must first pass a Resolve Test.

- **Passed:** it may march.
- **Failed:** it does not march, but may move normally. It still counts as having marched,
  even if you then do not move it at all.

### The battlefield edge

No unit may move off the battlefield, except fleeing and pursuing units. A corner of a unit may
pass over the edge during a manoeuvre, as long as the whole unit is back on the battlefield
when its move ends.

## Terrain and movement

Chapter 11 describes the seven kinds of terrain: open ground, hills, difficult terrain,
dangerous terrain, impassable terrain, linear obstacles (low and high) and woods. Before the
battle, agree with your opponent which kind each feature on the table is. This is how each kind
affects movement.

| Terrain | Effect on movement |
|---|---|
| Open ground | No effect. Every part of the table without a terrain feature is open ground. |
| Hills | Open ground, unless the players agree otherwise. |
| Decoration less than 2" across | No effect; models move straight through it. |
| Difficult terrain | Movement −1 (to no less than 1). |
| Dangerous terrain | As difficult terrain, plus a Dangerous Terrain test for each model. |
| Low linear obstacle (under 2" high: walls, hedges) | Counts as difficult terrain. |
| High linear obstacle (2" high or more: castle walls) | Counts as impassable terrain. |
| Woods | Difficult or dangerous terrain, as the players agree. |
| Impassable terrain | Cannot be crossed; units go around it. |

**Difficult terrain.** Should even part of a unit cross difficult ground, the whole unit's
Movement drops by 1, to no less than 1. It does not matter whether the unit starts in the
terrain, crosses it, or finishes in it. When at least a quarter (25%) of its models finish the
move inside difficult terrain, or astride a low linear obstacle, the unit becomes **Disrupted**.

**Dangerous terrain.** It slows a unit exactly as difficult terrain does. On top of that, every
model that starts its move in dangerous terrain, goes through it, or finishes in it takes a
**Dangerous Terrain test**. Roll a D6 per model: a 2+ means no harm, and a 1 means the model loses 1 Hit
Point. A model tests once for each separate dangerous feature it meets in a single move.

For charging through terrain, see Step 2; for fleeing through it, see Fleeing.
