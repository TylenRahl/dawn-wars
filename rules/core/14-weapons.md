# 14. Weapons and Armour

This chapter gives the common weapons and armour that any army may use. Weapons found in only
one army are in that army's list.

Every model in a unit normally carries the same kit. Members of the command group may carry
different weapons so that they are easy to pick out.

## Reading a weapon profile

A weapon has a profile, just as a model does: its characteristics and any special rules it
gives. For example:

| Weapon | Range | Mi | Pierce | Special rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flail | Combat | Mi+2 | -2 | Requires Two Hands, Armour Bane (1) |

*Notes: the Might bonus and Armour Bane (1) work only on enemies that this model charged in the
current turn.*

### Range

- **Combat:** a melee weapon, used only against enemies the wielder is engaged with.
- **A number of inches:** a missile weapon, shot at the enemy. The number is its maximum range.
- **Two numbers of inches:** a minimum and a maximum range. A stone thrower at 12-60" can only
  shoot at things from 12" to 60" away; anything nearer than 12" is too close.

### Might (Mi)

The Might used when rolling to wound. A missile weapon gives a plain number. A melee weapon may
show **Mi**, meaning the wielder's own Might, or Mi with a bonus such as **Mi+2**, meaning the
wielder's Might plus that much.

### Pierce

How well the weapon gets through armour. It is always a minus, taken off the target's Armour
Roll (chapter 5).

### Special rules

Many weapons pass one of the universal special rules (chapter 13) on to the attacks made with
them.

### Notes

A weapon's notes say when it may be used (for example, only in a turn its wielder charged), when
its bonuses count (for example, only in the first round of combat), and who may carry it (for
example, cavalry only). Rules unique to the weapon are given here too.

## Melee weapons

| Weapon | Range | Mi | Pierce | Special rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand weapon | Combat | Mi | - | - |
| Two hand weapons (additional hand weapon) | Combat | Mi | - | Requires Two Hands, Extra Attacks (+1) |
| Great weapon | Combat | Mi+2 | -2 | Requires Two Hands, Strike Last, Armour Bane (1) |
| Halberd | Combat | Mi+1 | -1(-2) | Requires Two Hands, Armour Bane (1) |
| Flail | Combat | Mi+2 | -2 | Requires Two Hands, Armour Bane (1) |
| Morning star | Combat | Mi+1 | -1 | Armour Bane (1) |
| Whip | Combat | Mi | - | Strike First, Fight in Extra Rank |
| Lance | Combat | Mi+2 | -2 | Armour Bane (1) |
| Cavalry spear | Combat | Mi+1 | -1 | Fight in Extra Rank |
| Thrusting spear | Combat | Mi | - | Fight in Extra Rank |
| Throwing spear | Combat | Mi | - | Fight in Extra Rank |

- **Hand weapon.** A sword, an axe, a club, or a beast's own teeth and claws. Unless its rules
  say otherwise, every model, cavalry mounts included, has a hand weapon.
- **Two hand weapons.** One weapon in each hand.
- **Great weapon.** Any big weapon swung with both hands: great axes, great hammers, two-handed
  swords and the like.
- **Halberd.** Its Pierce is -2, not -1, against enemies this model charged in the current turn.
- **Flail.** The Might bonus and Armour Bane (1) work only on enemies this model charged in the
  current turn.
- **Morning star.** A one-handed flail. The Might bonus and Armour Bane (1) likewise work only on
  enemies it charged in the current turn.
- **Lance.** Cavalry and monsters only. Usable only when the wielder charged this turn,
  and its Might and Pierce bonuses work only on the enemies it charged.
- **Cavalry spear.** Cavalry, monsters and chariots only. Its Might and Pierce bonuses apply only
  when the wielder charged this turn, and in that same turn it makes no supporting attack.
- **Thrusting spear.** Infantry only. No supporting attack in a turn when the wielder
  charged. When an enemy charges it in the front arc, it gets +1 Reflexes (Rf) that turn against
  the unit or units that charged.
- **Throwing spear.** Usable only when the wielder charged this turn; the spears are hurled
  over the front rank by those behind. In later turns, or if the model did not charge, it fights
  with its hand weapon.

| Brace of repeater handbows | 12" | 3 | - | Quick Shot, Multiple Shots (4) |

| Javelin | 12" | Mi | - | Quick Shot, Move & Shoot |
| Throwing axe | 9" | Mi+1 | - | Quick Shot |
| Throwing weapon | 9" | Mi | - | Quick Shot, Move & Shoot, Multiple Shots (2) |
| Handgun | 24" | 4 | -1 | Ponderous, Armour Bane (1) |
| Repeater handgun | 24" | 4 | -1 | Ponderous, Multiple Shots (3), Armour Bane (1) |
| Pistol | 12" | 4 | -1 | Quick Shot, Armour Bane (1) |
| Repeater pistol | 12" | 4 | -1 | Quick Shot, Multiple Shots (3), Armour Bane (1) |

| Brace of pistols (melee) | Combat | Mi | - | Requires Two Hands, Extra Attacks (+1) |

- **Bows** come as shortbows, warbows and longbows. A warbow uses the archer's own Might.
- **Thrown weapons** are javelins, throwing axes and throwing weapons (knives, stars and similar
  small blades).
- **Black powder weapons** are the handgun, repeater handgun, pistol, repeater pistol and brace
  of pistols.
- **Brace of pistols.** It has two profiles: one for shooting, one for use in melee.

## More than one weapon

### Melee weapons

When a unit carries two or more melee weapons and is engaged in combat, choose which one it fights
with each time its combat is picked, at step 1.1 of every Choose & Fight Combat sub-phase
(chapter 6).

### Missile weapons

When a unit carries two or more missile weapons, choose which one it shoots with when you pick it
in step 1 of the Fire Phase (chapter 5).

### Who must match

In either case the whole unit, command group included, uses the same weapon. For melee weapons a
unit with the Motley Crew rule may be an exception (chapter 13).

- A character may always choose for himself, whatever the unit he has joined is using.
- A unit champion who may be armed differently from his unit also chooses on his own.

## Armour

Heavier armour gives a model a better **armour value**, and the easier its Armour Roll
(chapter 5). Some creatures wear none and rely on a thick hide or scales instead.

A few models have an armour value written on their profile. Most take it from what they wear:

| Armour | Armour value |
|---|---|
| Full plate armour | 4+ |
| Heavy armour | 5+ |
| Light armour | 6+ |
| No armour | 7+ (see below) |

- **Light armour:** partial mail, a few plates, a tough hide.
- **Heavy armour:** a full coat of mail or padded leather, or thick scales.
- **Full plate armour:** plate from head to foot, or skin hard as steel.
- **No armour.** A model wearing nothing still counts as having an armour value of 7+ when you
  apply Pierce and anything that improves armour. So a model with no armour and a shield has
  6+.

### Unusual armour

Many armies have their own materials or spells that make armour better still. These are usually
given as a special rule in that army's list.

### Shields and barding

These two pieces of extra kit each improve the armour value by 1:

- **Shield.** Light armour gives 6+; light armour and a shield give 5+. A model fighting with a
  weapon that has Requires Two Hands cannot use a shield at the same time.
- **Barding.** Armour for a mount. A rider whose mount is barded has its armour value made 1 better: a
  cavalryman in heavy armour has 5+, and 4+ if his mount is barded.

### Best possible armour value

However much it wears or carries, no model's armour value can go past a limit set by its troop
type. Anything that would push it further has no effect.

| Troop type | Best armour value |
|---|---|
| Infantry, cavalry | 2+ |
| Chariot, monster, war machine | 3+ |

### Wizards and armour

A Wizard may not wear armour. A Wizard who does so cannot make casting rolls or dispel rolls
(chapter 9). This covers every kind of armour and shield, magical or not. Barding does not count,
since the mount wears it, not the Wizard.
