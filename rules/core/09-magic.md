# 9. Magic

Wizards bend the power of **the Weave** to cast spells. A spell can strengthen your own troops,
weaken the enemy, move units, or strike the foe outright. Your opponent's Wizards will try to
undo your spells, and you will try to undo theirs.

This chapter covers how Wizards learn, cast and dispel spells. The spells themselves are in the
lores, which are listed separately. New players can leave this chapter until they have played a
game or two without magic.

## The Weave and the Eight Threads

All magic in the game is drawn from the Weave. The Weave is made of **the Eight Threads**; each
one, a **Thread of the Weave**, has its own character, and the lores of magic are built around
them. Most of the time the Threads matter only in the background. The optional rules at the end
of this chapter, under **The Weave in Storm**, give them a direct part in play.

## Wizards

Any model that can cast spells is a **Wizard**, whatever its army calls it: priest, witch,
shaman, seer. All of them follow the rules in this chapter.

### Levels of Wizardry

Every Wizard has a **Level of Wizardry** (in these rules, just its **Level**) from 1 to 4. The
higher the Level, the more spells the Wizard knows, the more it can attempt each turn, and the
better its casting and dispelling.

| Level | The Wizard is... |
|---|---|
| 1 | a novice with modest gifts |
| 2 | a practised spellcaster |
| 3 | a rare and powerful adept |
| 4 | among the greatest Wizards alive |

### Wizards and armour

A Wizard may not wear armour or carry a shield. A Wizard that does so anyway can make **no
Casting rolls and no Dispel rolls**. This covers every kind of armour and shield, magical or
mundane. Barding is not included: it is worn by the mount, not the Wizard.

Some special rules and some magical armour lift this restriction. Where they do, their own rules
say so (for example, a special rule in the Dominion Hosts list).

## Lores and spells

### Lores of magic

A **lore** is a list of spells built around one approach to magic. Each Wizard's rules name the
lore or lores it can use. Some Wizards know a single lore. Others may pick from several: if so,
choose one when you write your muster list (chapter 12), and write it down.

### Generating spells

Spells are generated before deployment. If you have several Wizards, do them one at a time in
any order you like.

Every lore holds seven spells: six numbered 1 to 6, and a **signature spell**.

1. Roll one D6 for each Level of the Wizard. A Level 3 Wizard rolls three dice.
2. Re-roll any die that shows the same number as another. A Wizard can never know one spell
   twice.
3. The Wizard knows the numbered spells shown.
4. If you wish, the Wizard may give up one of those spells and take the lore's signature spell
   in its place. Any or all of your Wizards may do this.

Two Wizards in the same army may well know the same spell. That is allowed.

## Spell categories

Each spell belongs to one of six categories. The category decides when it can be cast and
what it may target.

| Category | Cast when | May target |
|---|---|---|
| **Enchantment** | Your Strategy Phase, Conjuration sub-phase | friendly units |
| **Hex** | Your Strategy Phase, Conjuration sub-phase | enemy units |
| **Conveyance** | Any time in the Remaining Moves sub-phase of your Manoeuvre Phase | friendly units |
| **Magic Missile** | When you choose the Wizard in your Fire Phase | enemy units, in line of sight |
| **Magical Vortex** | When you choose the Wizard in your Fire Phase | no target; uses a template |
| **Assailment** | When the Wizard fights, in any Melee Phase | enemy units it is fighting |

The sub-phases are described in chapter 3.

### Enchantment

Enchantments help your own side. One that needs a target must pick a friendly unit, though now
and then its effect also reaches enemies. Putting the same Enchantment on the same unit twice
in one turn does nothing more.

### Hex

Hexes hinder the enemy. One that needs a target must pick an enemy unit, though now and then its
effect also reaches friends. Putting the same Hex on the same unit twice in one turn does
nothing more.

Different Hexes and Enchantments **do** stack with each other, but no characteristic can be
raised above 10 or lowered below 1 by them.

### Conveyance

Conveyance spells speed units up or carry them across the table. One that needs a target must
pick a friendly unit. No unit can be affected by one Conveyance spell more than once in a turn.

### Magic Missile

A bolt flung at the enemy. The target must be an enemy unit, and the Wizard needs line of sight
to it. If the spell is cast, it hits automatically: no roll to hit.

### Magical Vortex

A rolling mass of power that crosses the table. A Vortex has no target. Instead you place a
round template on the table with its centre hole within the spell's range, and with the template
touching no model's base. It stays on the table after that, unless it moves off the edge.

Some Vortexes move at the start of every turn, often by scattering. If one finishes such a move
on top of a unit, shift it the shortest distance possible, in whatever direction, so that it
touches no model's base.

### Assailment

A sudden strike in close quarters. Only a Wizard engaged in combat can cast an Assailment, and
only at an enemy unit it is engaged with. If the spell is cast, it hits automatically: no roll
to hit.

## Range and targets

Unless a spell or its category says otherwise:

- The target must be **within the Wizard's vision arc**. Line of sight is **not** needed.
- The target must be **within the spell's range**.
- A unit that is **engaged in combat cannot be targeted**.

Magic Missiles (line of sight needed) and Assailments (only the unit being fought) are the main
exceptions, and some single spells add rules of their own.

### Spells with range Self

A spell whose range is **Self** has no target: it works on the Wizard itself. Some Self spells
spread out as an aura, measured from the Wizard. A Wizard can cast a Self spell while engaged in
combat.

## Casting spells

### How many spells

Each turn, a Wizard can **attempt** as many spells as its Level, and each spell only **once**.
A Level 2 Wizard who knows three spells can try two of them each turn.

A Wizard engaged in combat can cast only Assailments and Self spells, unless a rule says
otherwise.

### Casting a spell

1. **Declare.** Say which spell the Wizard is casting and, if it needs one, its target.
2. **Casting roll.** Roll 2D6.
3. **Casting result.** Add half the Wizard's Level, rounding up (+1 at Level 1 or 2, +2 at
   Level 3 or 4), to the Casting roll.
4. **Compare.** Every spell has a **casting value**. Bigger spells have higher values.
   - Casting result **at least** the casting value: the spell is **cast**. Your
     opponent may now try to dispel it.
   - Casting result **lower**: the spell **fails**, and nothing happens.

Leave the dice where they lie until any dispel attempt is over. Your opponent needs your
casting result to beat.

*Example: a Level 3 Wizard rolls a 4 and a 3 for a spell with casting value 9. The Casting roll
is 7; adding 2 for Level 3 gives a casting result of 9. The spell is cast.*

### Perfect invocation

A **natural double 6** on the Casting roll is a **perfect invocation**: the spell succeeds.
It is cast whatever its casting value, and whatever modifiers apply to the roll. A perfect
invocation **cannot be dispelled** by any means, not even an unbinding.

### Backlash

If the Casting roll is a **natural double 1**, the spell suffers a **Backlash**, whatever the
casting result. Unless a rule says otherwise, the spell is not cast. Roll 2D6 on the Backlash
table straight away.

#### Backlash table

| 2D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 2-4 | **Weave Storm.** Centre a 5" blast template on the Wizard. Every model whose base is under it, friend or foe, risks one hit at Might 10, Pierce -4. |
| 5-6 | **Burst.** Centre a 3" blast template on the Wizard. Every model whose base is under it, friend or foe, risks one hit at Might 6, Pierce -2. |
| 7 | **Stumble.** The Wizard takes one hit at Might 4, Pierce -1. |
| 8-9 | **Strained Grip.** The spell is cast. For any dispel attempt, treat its casting result as equal to its casting value. The Wizard can attempt no more spells this turn. |
| 10-12 | **Drained Calm.** The spell goes off as a perfect invocation. After it, **none of your Wizards** can attempt any more spells this turn. |

Note the difference between 8-9 and 10-12: Strained Grip stops only this Wizard, Drained Calm
stops your whole army.

How a blast template finds the models it hits is in chapter 5.

## Dispelling spells

Each time your opponent casts a spell, you may try to dispel it, unless it was a perfect
invocation.

### Wizardly or Fated

Before rolling, choose which kind of dispel you are attempting.

**Wizardly dispel.** Pick one of your Wizards within **Dispel range** of the Wizard who cast the
spell:

| Your Wizard's Level | Dispel range |
|---|---|
| 1 or 2 | 18" |
| 3 or 4 | 24" |

A Wizard that is fleeing, or is not on the table, cannot be picked. A Wizard engaged in combat
can be picked only against a spell that targets it or its own unit.

**Fated dispel.** Once per turn you may leave it to chance instead: no Wizard is used. You might
do this because none of your Wizards can make a Wizardly dispel, or simply to avoid the risk of
one.

### The Dispel roll

1. **Dispel roll.** Roll 2D6.
2. **Dispel result.** For a Wizardly dispel, add half the dispelling Wizard's Level, rounding
   up. For a Fated dispel, add nothing.
3. **Compare** with the casting result:
   - Dispel result **higher**: the spell is **dispelled** and ends at once.
   - Dispel result **equal or lower**: the dispel fails and the spell works.

A tie goes to the caster.

### Unbinding

A **natural double 6** on any Dispel roll, Wizardly or Fated, is an **unbinding**. The spell is
dispelled and ends at once, whatever the casting result. An unbinding still cannot touch a
perfect invocation.

### Outclassed in the art

A **natural double 1** on a **Wizardly** Dispel roll means the dispelling Wizard is
**outclassed in the art**. Roll at once on the Backlash table for that Wizard. Read every
mention of casting on the table as dispelling, and every mention of a perfect invocation as an
unbinding.

## Resolving the spell

When a spell has been cast and has not been dispelled, whether the dispel failed or none was
tried, carry out its effect. Each spell's own text says what it does.

## How long spells last

Most spells take effect the moment they are cast, and that is the end of them until they are
cast again. Others last a while: some until the end of the caster's turn, some until the next
round, running out at the start of the caster's next Start of Turn sub-phase. The spell's text
says which.

### Remains in Play

A spell marked **Remains in Play** lasts with no fixed end. It stops only when:

- the Wizard who cast it is slain;
- the Wizard leaves the table; or
- the Wizard chooses to end it, which it may do as any sub-phase begins.

Keeping it going takes little effort, so the Wizard may go on casting other spells. It may not
cast the same Remains in Play spell again while it lasts.

### Dispelling a Remains in Play spell later

If you did not dispel an enemy Remains in Play spell when it was cast, you get further chances:
in the **Conjuration sub-phase of each of your own later turns**, you may try again. Fated and
Wizardly dispels are both allowed. For a Wizardly dispel, your Wizard must be within Dispel range
of the casting Wizard, or, for a Magical Vortex, within Dispel range of its template.

The spell has lost the strength of its first casting. Your dispel result now has to beat the
**casting value** printed in the spell, not the casting result that was rolled.

A perfect invocation of a Remains in Play spell is safe on the turn it is cast, but it can be
dispelled this way in later turns.

### The Conjuration sub-phase

During your Conjuration sub-phase (Strategy Phase, chapter 3), pick one of your Wizards that is
not fleeing. It may attempt any Enchantments and Hexes it knows; after each one that is cast,
your opponent may try to dispel it. Then pick another Wizard, and go on until each of your
Wizards has had its chance.

## Bound spells

Some models have **Bound spells**, held in a magic item or granted by a special rule. A model
with a Bound spell is **not** a Wizard because of it.

- A Bound spell is cast like any other spell, with a Casting roll of 2D6.
- If the Bound spell has a **Power Level**, add it to the Casting roll for the casting result.
  If it has none, the Casting roll alone is the casting result.
- A Bound spell can never suffer a Backlash and can never be a perfect invocation.
- A model may cast **one** Bound spell per phase.
- Your opponent may try to dispel a Bound spell in the normal way. Magic items that dispel or
  destroy spells affect Bound spells just as they affect others. A Wizard dispelling a Bound spell can never be
  outclassed in the art.

## Magic Resistance

A model with **Magic Resistance (-X)** shrugs off hostile magic. When any spell, Bound spells
included, targets an enemy unit with at least one such model in it, its Casting roll takes a
modifier of -X, the number in brackets.

Magic Resistance does not add up. If several models in the unit have it, use the single biggest
modifier.

## The Weave in Storm (optional rules)

These rules make magic stronger and riskier. They are optional: agree before the game which of
them, if any, you are using. Three sets are offered:

- **Channelling**: each Wizard gets a pool of dice to spend on its magic.
- **Gambits**: each player gets a hand of one-use tricks.
- **Hidden Arts**: Wizards may buy a single secret ability each.

Use either Channelling or Gambits, not both. Hidden Arts go with either of them, or alone. An
organised event should follow the same limits and tell players in good time which rules it uses.

### The Severe Backlash table

If you are using Channelling or Gambits, roll every Backlash on this table instead of the normal
one. As before, the spell fails unless the result says otherwise.

Players who want magic to be more dangerous may use this table even without Channelling or
Gambits.

| 2D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 2-3 | **Torn Weave.** Roll a scatter dice. On an arrow, lay a flame template pointing the way the arrow shows, its narrow tip on the middle of the Wizard's base. On a Hit, centre a 5" blast template on the Wizard instead. Every model under the template, friend or foe, apart from the Wizard, risks one hit at Might 10, Pierce -4. After those hits, the Wizard is reduced to 0 Hit Points and removed. |
| 4-5 | **Weave Storm.** Centre a 5" blast template on the Wizard. Every model under it, friend or foe, risks one hit at Might 8, Pierce -3. The Wizard can attempt no more spells this turn. |
| 6-7 | **Burst.** Centre a 3" blast template on the Wizard. Every model under it, friend or foe, risks one hit at Might 6, Pierce -2. |
| 8-9 | **Recoil.** The Wizard takes one hit at Might 4, Pierce -1, and is **Bewildered** for the rest of the game. |
| 10-11 | **Blown Back.** The Wizard becomes **Bewildered**. It also Gives Ground at once (chapter 7), with any unit it has joined, straight away from the closest enemy unit. If Giving Ground is impossible, the Wizard takes one hit at Might 6, Pierce -2 instead. |
| 12 | **Price Paid.** The spell goes off as a perfect invocation. The Wizard loses 1 Level and forgets the spell it just cast for the rest of the game. None of your Wizards can attempt any more spells this turn. |

**Bewildered.** A Bewildered Wizard has -1 on its Casting rolls and Dispel rolls. Each further
time it becomes Bewildered the penalty grows by 1: -2, then -3, and so on.

### Channelling

**Building the pool.** Channelling dice are made before deployment, just before spells are
generated. Take one Wizard, roll for its dice below, and set that many dice aside as its
**Channelling pool**. Then generate that Wizard's spells, and repeat for your next Wizard. You
can keep a written tally instead of a pile of dice.

| Level | Channelling dice |
|---|---|
| 1 | D3 |
| 2 | D3+1 |
| 3 | 2D3 |
| 4 | 2D3+1 |

**Spending.** A Wizard may spend as many of its Channelling dice in a turn as it likes. Each one
spent leaves the pool for good. They buy:

- **Channelled spells (1 die).** While generating spells, before swapping one for the signature
  spell, re-roll one of the Wizard's spell dice (duplicates are re-rolled as usual). The new
  result stands, even if it is the same spell.
- **Channelled dispel (1 die).** When this Wizard makes a Wizardly dispel, including against a
  Remains in Play spell from an earlier turn, roll one extra D6 for the Dispel roll and drop the
  lowest. A natural double 1 or double 6 counts if it shows on **any two** of the dice.
- **Channelled luck (2 dice).** When this Wizard fails a Casting roll or a Dispel roll, re-roll
  it. This cannot be used to escape a Backlash.
- **Reach (1 die).** Before choosing a spell's target, or before making a Wizardly dispel, roll
  the Channelling die and add that many inches to the range of the spell, or to the Wizard's
  Dispel range.

### Gambits

Gambits are one-use tricks that a player saves up and spends during the game. They come from a
deck of 56 Gambit cards or from a 2D6 table holding the same tricks.

**Getting your gambits.** At the start of the game, after spells are generated, each player gets
six gambits: draw the top six cards of a shuffled deck, or roll six times on the table.

- If each player has a deck, each draws from their own.
- If only one deck is available, share it, taking turns to draw one card at a time. A player who
  draws more copies of a card than the table allows puts the extra back, reshuffles, and draws
  again.
- If no one has a deck, both players roll on the table.

Both players must use the same method, and must draw or roll where the other can see.

| 2D6 | Gambit | Most per player |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Skyward Casting | 1 |
| 3 | Far Unravelling | 2 |
| 4 | Echo | 4 |
| 5 | Second Fall | no limit |
| 6 | Steady Unweaving | no limit |
| 7 | Recast | no limit |
| 8 | Second Unweaving | no limit |
| 9 | Surge | no limit |
| 10 | Bold Gamble | 4 |
| 11 | Clean Unweaving | 2 |
| 12 | Perfect Moment | 1 |

When rolling, re-roll any result you already hold the most of.

**Using gambits.** Any model that fits a gambit's description may use it: a Wizard, a Deephold
Runesmith, or a model carrying a Bound spell. Some of the dispel gambits also work with a Fated
dispel, as their text says. Each player may use **one gambit per phase**. A used gambit is
discarded for the rest of the game.

- **Skyward Casting.** Use when a model is about to cast a spell or Bound spell, before naming
  the target. It may target a unit out of line of sight, and even beyond the spell's maximum
  range.
- **Far Unravelling.** Use when you want to make a Wizardly dispel. Pick any one of your Wizards
  to make it, ignoring Dispel range, even if that Wizard is engaged in combat. A Wizard that is
  fleeing or suffering from Stupidity cannot be picked.
- **Echo.** Use straight after a model casts a spell or Bound spell, even if it was then
  dispelled. The model may cast that spell a second time at once, at the same unit or a
  different one. This attempt does not count against the Wizard's limit for the turn.
- **Second Fall.** Use after one of your Wizards has rolled on the Backlash table. Re-roll that
  roll; the new result stands even if it is worse.
- **Steady Unweaving.** Use when you make a Wizardly or Fated dispel. Roll one extra D6 for the
  Dispel roll and drop the lowest. If a double 1 shows, you may drop one of the 1s to avoid
  being outclassed in the art.
- **Recast.** Use when one of your Wizards fails a Casting roll or rolls a Backlash: it re-rolls
  the Casting roll. Or use it to re-roll a failed Casting roll for a model casting a Bound spell.
- **Second Unweaving.** Use when one of your Wizards fails a Dispel roll, outclassed in the art
  included: it re-rolls the Dispel roll. Or use it to re-roll a failed Fated dispel.
- **Surge.** Use when one of your Wizards casts a spell. Roll one extra D6 for the Casting roll
  and drop the lowest. If a double 1 shows, you may drop one of the 1s to avoid the Backlash.
- **Bold Gamble.** Use after one of your Wizards suffers a Backlash, before rolling on the
  table. Roll on it twice and pick which result happens.
- **Clean Unweaving.** Use when you make a Wizardly or Fated dispel. The spell is dispelled
  automatically; no Dispel roll is made.
- **Perfect Moment.** Use after a model rolls any natural double except double 1 on a Casting
  roll, Bound spells included. If its casting result beat the spell's casting value,
  it becomes a perfect invocation.

### Hidden Arts

A Hidden Art is a single secret ability a Wizard can buy.

**Buying.** Any Wizard may buy one roll on the Hidden Arts table for **+25 points**, at most one
per Wizard.

**Rolling.** Roll for Hidden Arts just before spells are generated: one D6 for each Wizard that
bought one. If both players have them, take turns to roll, each in plain view of the other.
Write down each result and which Wizard has it.

| D6 | Hidden Art |
|---|---|
| 1 | Escapology |
| 2 | Soul Rend |
| 3 | Spell Thief |
| 4 | Mirror Turn |
| 5 | Weave Drain |
| 6 | Spell Eater |

**Using.** A Wizard uses its Hidden Art when the description allows. After one use, whether it
worked or not, it is gone.

Most Hidden Arts are a **duel**: when an enemy Wizard casts a spell, your Wizard uses its Art
**in place of** a Wizardly dispel. Each Art says what both Wizards roll and add. Then:

- **Enemy total higher:** the Art fails and the spell goes ahead.
- **Totals equal:** the spell is dispelled.
- **Your total higher:** the spell is dispelled and the Art's full effect happens.

The Arts:

- **Escapology.** The first time this Wizard drops to 0 Hit Points, just before it is removed,
  roll a D6. On 1-3 nothing happens and it is removed. On 4+ it gets back 1 lost Hit Point and
  stays.
- **Soul Rend** (duel). Your Wizard rolls D6 + its Level. The enemy Wizard rolls D6 + its
  Resilience. If you win, the spell is dispelled and the enemy caster loses 1 Hit Point. No
  Regeneration save can be taken against that loss.
- **Spell Thief** (duel). Each Wizard rolls D6 and adds half its Level, rounding up. If you win,
  the spell is dispelled, and its caster can never try to cast it again this game.
- **Mirror Turn** (duel). Your Wizard rolls D6 + its Level. The enemy Wizard rolls D6 + its
  Reflexes. If you win, the spell turns back on its caster: resolve it against the casting
  Wizard and its unit as though they were its target.
- **Weave Drain** (duel). Your Wizard rolls 2D6 + its Level. The enemy Wizard rolls D6 + its
  Resolve. If you win, the spell is dispelled, and so is every other enemy spell in play at that
  moment.
- **Spell Eater.** Use this in place of a Wizardly dispel against an enemy spell.
  The spell is dispelled automatically, with no roll. Against a perfect invocation it is not
  automatic: you may make a Dispel roll, and a natural double 6 dispels it. Either way, the next
  Casting roll your Wizard makes after this may roll one extra D6 and drop the lowest. If a
  double 1 shows, it may drop one of the 1s to avoid a Backlash.
