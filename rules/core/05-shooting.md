# 5. The Fire Phase

In the Fire Phase your bows, guns, war machines and battle spells go to work. This chapter covers
ordinary missile weapons and the Wizards who cast magic missiles and magical vortexes. War
machines use most of these rules too, with extra rules of their own in chapter 10.

## How the phase works

You shoot with one unit at a time. Pick a unit, take it through all four steps below, then pick
another. Carry on until every unit that can shoot (or cast) has had its chance.

1. **Pick a unit and its target.** Choose one of your units that can shoot. Check which enemy
   units it can see and reach, then name the one it will shoot at.
2. **Roll to hit.** Roll for every model that is shooting, applying any modifiers. Not every
   model in the unit may be able to fire.
3. **Roll to wound, then Armour Rolls.** Roll to wound with every hit. Your opponent then gets an
   Armour Roll for each wound.
4. **Take casualties, then Panic.** Every wound that is not saved costs the target 1 Hit Point.
   Models left on 0 HP are removed. Heavy losses force a Panic test.

**Spells.** A Wizard of yours who knows a magic missile or magical vortex spell may try to cast it
when you pick him in this phase (see chapter 9). A magic missile needs line of sight to an enemy
unit and hits without any roll to hit. A magical vortex needs no target: its round template goes
down with its centre hole within the spell's range, touching no model's base, and it stays on
the table until it leaves it. If a vortex that moves (often by scattering) at the start of a turn
would end over a unit, shift it the shortest distance, any direction, until it touches no base.

## 1. Pick a unit and its target

### Who can shoot

A unit can shoot only if it has missile weapons (war machines count) or a Wizard with a spell of
a type that is cast in this phase, such as a magic missile. Even then, it cannot shoot if:

- it charged or marched in the Manoeuvre Phase just gone,
- it is engaged in combat, or
- it is fleeing.

### How many shots

Each unit shoots once per Fire Phase. Most models fire a single shot. A model's Strikes (St) has
nothing to do with how many shots it gets.

### Which ranks fire

Normally only the **front rank** shoots. Two things change that:

- **On a hill.** A unit standing on a hill fires with one more rank than it would on flat ground.
  A unit of crossbowmen on a hill, for example, shoots with its first two ranks.
- **Volley Fire.** A weapon with this rule lets half the models in every rank behind the front
  rank (behind the front two, on a hill) shoot over their comrades. They fire as well as the
  usual front rank or ranks. Volley Fire is not allowed if the unit moved for any reason this
  turn (reforming included) or when it Stands and Shoots.

Whenever you have to work out how many models can fire, round up.

### Line of sight

A model can only shoot at what it can see. Get down to the model's eye level:

- If you can trace a straight, unbroken line from inside the model's vision arc to some part of
  the other model, it has line of sight.
- If terrain is in the way, or the line would pass over or through another model, it does not.

A model is always inside its own vision arc and can always see itself and its own unit. A unit
can see another unit if at least one of its models can see at least one model of the other.

Every model and unit blocks sight, so as a rule only the front rank can see. Check line
of sight separately for every model in the front rank. When rules let rear ranks shoot, a model
in a rear rank sees whatever the front model of its file sees.

### Range

Every missile weapon has a maximum range, and a model cannot shoot at a target beyond it. Measure
for each model on its own: some may reach while others do not.

The one exception is a unit that **Stands and Shoots** against a charge (chapter 4). Its shots
assume the chargers have already come into range.

### Naming the target

Once you know who can see and reach what, declare which enemy unit the shooters aim at. A unit
cannot split its fire. Any model in it that cannot see the target, or is out of range of it,
simply does not shoot.

### Enemies in combat

As a rule, you cannot shoot at an enemy unit that is engaged in combat; your own fighters are too
close to it. Only a rule that says otherwise lets you.

## 2. Roll to hit

Roll one D6 for each model shooting. Find the shooter's Marksmanship (Mk) on the chart. A roll
that equals or beats the number shown, after modifiers, is a hit.

| Marksmanship | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Score needed | 6+ | 5+ | 4+ | 3+ | 2+ |

A **natural 1 always misses**, whatever the modifiers.

### Marksmanship 6 or more

A shooter with Mk 6 or better may re-roll a miss. The re-roll is usually harder to pass. The
first number below is for the first roll, the second for the re-roll:

| Marksmanship | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First roll | 2+ | 2+ | 2+ | 2+ | 2+ |
| Re-roll | 6+ | 5+ | 4+ | 3+ | 2+ |

### Modifiers to hit

All of these add together unless a rule says otherwise. Others exist, but these are the ones you
will meet most:

| Situation | Modifier |
|---|---|
| Shooter moved this turn | -1 |
| Target beyond half the weapon's range (long range) | -1 |
| Shooter is Standing and Shooting | -1 |
| Target in partial cover | -1 |
| Target in full cover | -2 |

- **Moving.** A model that moved this turn for any reason, rallying and reforming included, takes
  -1.
- **Long range.** A shot at an enemy more than half the weapon's maximum range away takes -1.
- **Stand and Shoot.** Shots fired as a Stand and Shoot reaction take -1, but never the long
  range -1 on top of it.
- **Cover** depends on how much of the line of sight is blocked by terrain or other models.
  Against a lone model, count how much of the model is hidden; against a unit, count how many of
  its models are hidden.
  - Up to half hidden: **partial cover**, -1.
  - More than half hidden: **full cover**, -2.

For a shooter with Mk 6 or more, modifiers apply only to the first roll, never to the re-roll.

Modifiers often affect some shooters in a unit and not others. Roll those shots as a separate
group.

### Needing 7 or more

Enough minus modifiers can push the score you need past 6. A model with Mk 2 hits on 5+; with -2
it would need a 7. You can still hit: roll as usual, then roll again every die that showed a
natural 6. The second roll needed is:

| Score needed | A natural 6 hits if the second roll is |
|---|---|
| 7 | 4+ |
| 8 | 5+ |
| 9 | 6 |
| 10 | it cannot hit |

### Automatic hits

Some attacks just hit. Skip the roll to hit and go straight on.

### Rolling in batches

You need not roll one die at a time. Count the shooting models and throw that many dice together,
or in a few smaller handfuls if there are lots.

If the unit mixes models with different characteristics (Mk or Might, say), or weapons with
different profiles, the numbers needed may differ. Roll each group as its own batch, and tell
your opponent which batch is which and what it needs.

## 3. Roll to wound, then Armour Rolls

A hit is not yet a wound. Pick up every die that hit and roll it again. Compare the weapon's Might
(Mi, from its profile in chapter 14 or in the army list) with the target's Resilience (Re). A roll that equals or beats the number shown, after
modifiers, is a wound.

| Might compared with Resilience | Roll to wound |
|---|---|
| Might is 2 or more higher | 2+ |
| Might is 1 higher | 3+ |
| Might equals Resilience | 4+ |
| Might is 1 lower | 5+ |
| Might is 2 to 5 lower | 6+ |
| Might is 6 or more lower | cannot wound |

- A **natural 1 never wounds**, whatever the modifiers.
- **Too tough.** When the Might falls short of the Resilience by 6 or more, there is no roll
  that wounds.
- Keep the same batches you used to hit, and keep telling your opponent what each batch is and
  what it needs.

### Armour value

Most models get their **armour value** from what they wear (chapter 14); a few have one printed
on their profile. It is a score to roll, so lower is better. Light armour, for instance, gives
6+.

Other gear can improve it, but never past **2+**. A shield improves it by 1, so light armour and a
shield make 5+. A model with no armour counts as having 7+ when you apply Pierce or anything that
improves its armour value.

### Armour Rolls

For each wound, your opponent rolls a D6 for the wounded model: an **Armour Roll**.

- Equal to or above its armour value: the armour holds and the wound is ignored.
- Below it: the wound is **unsaved**.
- A **natural 1 always fails**, on any kind of Armour Roll, whatever the modifiers.

### Pierce

A weapon's **Pierce** is a minus applied to the target's Armour Roll. A model with armour value 5+
hit by a Pierce -1 weapon now needs a 6: a roll of 5 counts as a 4.

### Untouchable saves

An **Untouchable save** stands for some magical shield. Roll a D6 against the number given for it,
which is printed with whatever item or spell gives it, or in brackets after a special rule's name.
Rules that change armour values leave Untouchable saves alone, unless they say they don't.

A model with both makes its Armour Roll first. Only if that fails, or modifiers have made it
impossible to pass, does it roll its Untouchable save. A model gets one Untouchable save at most:
if it has several, it uses the best and never adds them together.

Regeneration saves are special rules; see chapter 13.

## 4. Take casualties, then Panic

### Losing Hit Points

Models take wounds from shooting, combat, spells and much else. Armour stops some of them. Each
wound that gets through costs the model 1 Hit Point (HP). At 0 HP the model is a casualty and is
taken off the table.

Hand out the unsaved wounds to the target unit one at a time. Stop when they are all used or the
unit is gone.

- **1 HP models.** Most models have a single HP and are removed as soon as they lose it.
- **Models with more HP.** Remove as many whole models as you can. One model takes the lost HP
  until it reaches 0, then the next model starts taking them. You may never spread the damage
  around to keep models alive. *Example: a unit of HP 3 brutes loses 5 HP. One brute loses all 3
  and is removed; a second loses 2 and is left on 1 HP.*

### Which models come off

Always take casualties from one end of the **back rank**, left to right or right to left as the
owner likes, keeping the models that remain next to each other wherever possible.

If the unit has only **one rank**, take casualties evenly from both ends of it.

**Stepping forward.** Casualties really fall in the front rank; taking them from the back stands
for the warriors behind moving up to fill the gaps. A model cannot attack at all,
shooting or in combat, in a phase in which it stepped forward this way.

### Multiple damage

Some weapons turn each unsaved wound into several. Roll to hit, to wound and Armour Rolls as
normal, then for every unsaved wound work out how many HP it costs and take them from the model
that was hit. A model cannot lose more HP than it has: it dies, and anything left over is lost.

### Instant kills

Some attacks do not wound; they simply remove the model. Unless the rule says otherwise, no save
of any kind is allowed, and the model's HP do not matter.

### Panic

A unit that has lost over a quarter (25%) of the models it began this Fire Phase with
takes a **Panic test** straight away: a Resolve Test against its Resolve (Rs). Pass, and it
holds. Fail, and it panics. When several of one army's units must test at once, their owner
picks the order.

What a panicked unit does depends on how many of its models are left, counted against what it
had when the battle began:

- **More than half left:** it **Falls Back in Good Order**, heading straight away from the
  enemy unit that shot it into testing.
- **Half or fewer left:** it **flees** at once, straight away from that same enemy unit.

A unit falling back in good order moves just like a fleeing unit, except that it rolls two D6
for its flee roll and keeps the higher (if they match, keep either), and it rallies by itself at
the end of the move. Fleeing is covered in chapter 7.

**One Panic test per phase.** A unit never takes more than one Panic test from shooting in the
same Fire Phase. If it loses over a quarter to one enemy unit and passes, more losses from a second
unit later that phase don't make it test again. Shots are resolved one unit at a time, but on the
battlefield they all fly together.

## Hits outside the Fire Phase

Some attacks and special rules cause hits in the Strategy Phase or the Manoeuvre Phase. Resolve
them with steps 3 and 4 of this chapter.
