# 2. Characteristics and Troop Types

Every miniature in the game is a **model**. Each model has a profile: a row of numbers that says
what it can do, and a block of other information (troop type, base, unit size, kit, special rules
and cost). This chapter explains every part of that profile, then the troop types, then the
formations a unit can stand in besides ordinary ranks.

## The model profile

### The characteristics

A profile has nine characteristics. Here is an ordinary line soldier:

**Line soldier:** M4, CM3, Mk3, Mi3, Re3, HP1, Rf3, St1, Rs7.

| Characteristic | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Movement (M)** | How far the model goes, in inches, in a normal move. M4 means up to 4". |
| **Combat Mastery (CM)** | Skill with weapons in melee, or a beast's ferocity. |
| **Marksmanship (Mk)** | Skill with bows, guns and other ranged weapons. |
| **Might (Mi)** | Raw power: how hard the model's blows land in melee. |
| **Resilience (Re)** | How well the model shrugs off injury and pain. |
| **Hit Points (HP)** | How much damage it takes to kill the model. A man-sized model usually has 1; great beasts and heroes have more. |
| **Reflexes (Rf)** | Speed of reaction. In melee, Reflexes sets the order in which models strike. |
| **Strikes (St)** | How many attacks the model makes in melee. Most have 1; veterans, monsters and heroes often have more. |
| **Resolve (Rs)** | Nerve and self-control. A low Resolve marks a skittish or cowardly creature. |

Every characteristic runs from 0 to 10. None can drop below 0, and going above 10 is very rare.

### Characteristics of zero

A value of **0** means the model simply cannot do that thing. The most common case is Marksmanship
0, for models with no way to shoot.

- A model (or object) with **Combat Mastery 0** cannot defend itself: every melee attack against
  it hits automatically.
- A model whose **Might, Resilience or Hit Points** falls to 0 at any time is slain and removed
  from play.

### Modifying characteristics

When a rule tells you to modify a characteristic, add or subtract the amount given. The result is
the model's new value for that characteristic.

### Characteristic tests

Some rules ask a model to test against one of its characteristics (a "Resilience test", a
"Reflexes test", and so on). Roll one D6:

- Roll **equal to or under** the characteristic: the test is passed.
- Roll **over** it: the test is failed.

Where a model or unit carries two or more values for the same characteristic, use the highest one for the test.

**Automatic results.** An unmodified 1 always passes and an unmodified 6 always fails, whatever
modifiers apply. A model whose characteristic is 0 or a dash (-) fails the test automatically.

### Resolve Tests

A Resolve Test is the exception: roll **2D6** instead of one die. A total equal to or under the
model's Resolve passes; a total above it fails. Failing a Resolve Test often makes a unit flee
(chapter 7).

A unit whose models have different Resolve values always uses the **highest** value among them,
here and whenever else Resolve is used: soldiers take heart from the steadiest among them.

**Automatic results.** A natural double 1 (a total of 2) always passes. A natural double 6 (a total
of 12) always fails, whatever modifiers apply.

### Split profiles

Some profiles have two or more rows, each with gaps shown as a dash (-). Each row is a separate
part of what plays as one model: a rider and a mount, say, or a chariot, its crew and its beasts.
How each kind of split profile works is set out under its troop type below.

## Other information on a profile

Besides its characteristics, a model's entry lists some or all of the following. Not every model
has every item (only Wizards have a Magic entry, for instance).

### Troop type

Every model has a troop type. There are five categories, each split into sub-categories:
**Infantry, Cavalry, Chariots, Monsters** and **War Machines**. They are explained in full later in
this chapter.

A troop type followed by "(Character)" marks a character, a leader or hero of the army. Characters have chapter 8 to themselves.

### Base size

Almost every model stands on a square or rectangular base. Its size is given in millimetres. Where
a range of sizes is given, use the base the model was supplied with.

### Unit size

This says how many models of this kind make up a unit:

- **A number and a plus**, such as **5+**: the unit needs at least that many models (here five),
  with no upper limit.
- **A range**, such as **5-30**: at least the first number, at most the second.
- **A single number**, usually **1**: exactly that many. This is the usual case for characters,
  chariots and monsters.

All your models are grouped into units before the battle starts, best done while you write your
muster list (chapter 12). A unit is normally several alike models standing together in one
formation, but a lone model also counts as a unit: a character, a chariot, a huge beast, a war
machine with its crew. Whenever the rules say "unit", that includes a unit of one model.

### Equipment

This lists the weapons and armour the model carries; their cost is already in its points value.
Beasts often list natural weapons here (claws, fangs, even a breath attack), which count as the
weapons they bring to the fight. The rules for weapons and armour are in chapter 14.

Normally every model in a unit carries the same kit. Command models may be armed differently so
they are easy to pick out.

**Armour value.** Most models get their armour value from the armour they wear (chapter 14). Big
models with several crew or riders, such as monsters and chariots, may instead have an armour
value stated in their own rules; where one is given, use it.

**Unique equipment and magic items.** A few models carry kit that no other model has, or magic
items of their own. Such items are described, with their rules, in the model's own entry.

### Magic

A model that can cast spells says so in its entry, along with the lore or lores it may take spells
from and its **Level of Wizardry**. There are four Levels, from 1 (the least able) to 4 (the most
powerful). The rules for magic and the Weave are in chapter 9.

### Special rules

On top of the rules that come with its troop type, a model may have special rules of three kinds:

- **Universal special rules** are shared by every army. Only their names appear in the model's
  entry; the rules themselves are in chapter 13.
- **Army special rules** belong to one army. Their names appear in the model's entry.
- **Unique special rules** belong to that model alone and are written out in its entry.

Army and unique special rules are given in each army list.

### Points value

Almost every model has a points value, which measures what it is worth to its army. A model starts
at a base cost, which goes up as you buy extra weapons, armour, magic items and the like. Add up
the cost of every model you pick and you have your army's total, which lets two players set up an
even fight. Building an army is covered in chapter 12.

### Options

Many models list options: other weapons they can carry, better armour, and so on. Most models that
form units can also promote some of their number to **command models**.

A unit's champion, standard bearer and musician together make up its **command group**. Many units
may take all three (a "full command"); others may take only one or two. Command models are always
bought as upgrades to ordinary models already in the unit, as the army list explains.

Unless a rule says otherwise, command models still count as **rank and file** models for any rule
that mentions rank and file.

## Troop types at a glance

A model's troop type, together with the formation its unit stands in, decides how it behaves under
almost every rule in this book. Each category has sub-categories. A rule that names a category also
covers all its sub-categories unless it makes an exception: a rule for Infantry applies to
Monstrous Infantry too, unless it says otherwise.

Troop type sets three numbers for a unit:

- **Models per rank**: the fewest models a rank needs to count towards the Rank Bonus. A rank may
  hold more or fewer models than this as you please, but it only adds to the Rank Bonus if it
  holds at least this many.
- **Maximum Rank Bonus**: the most the unit can claim. Special rules may raise it; formation,
  spells or terrain may lower it.
- **Unit Mass per model.**

### Troop type table

| Category | Sub-category | Unit Mass per model | Models per rank | Max Rank Bonus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry | Regular Infantry | 1 | 5 | +2 |
| Infantry | Heavy Infantry | 1 | 4 | +2 |
| Infantry | Monstrous Infantry | 3 | 3 | +2 |
| Infantry | Swarms | 3 | - | - |
| Cavalry | Light Cavalry | 2 | 5 | +1 |
| Cavalry | Heavy Cavalry | 2 | 4 | +1 |
| Cavalry | Monstrous Cavalry | 3 | 3 | +1 |
| Cavalry | War Beasts | 1 | 5 | +1 |
| Chariots | Light Chariots | 3 | 3 | +1 |
| Chariots | Heavy Chariots | 5 | - | - |
| Monsters | Monstrous Creatures | Starting HP | - | - |
| Monsters | Behemoths | Starting HP | - | - |
| War Machines | War Machines | Starting HP | - | - |

The special rules each sub-category gets are listed with it under Troop types in detail.

A dash means the troop type never claims a Rank Bonus. "Starting HP" means each model's Unit
Mass equals the Hit Points it began the battle with.

### Rank Bonus

A unit in a formation that allows it, and that is wide enough, claims **+1 for each full-enough
rank behind its first**, up to its troop type's maximum. A rear rank that is not complete still
counts, provided it holds at least the models-per-rank number. A **Disrupted** unit claims no Rank
Bonus at all (chapter 6).

### Unit Mass

Counting heads undersells big, savage creatures, so some rules (for example, checking whether a
unit is outnumbered) use **Unit Mass** instead. To find it, multiply the number of models left
in the unit by the Unit Mass per model from the table.

> **Example:** a unit of six Monstrous Infantry has a Unit Mass of 6 x 3 = 18.

## Troop types in detail

Telling which main category a model belongs to is usually easy. The sub-categories are finer, and
exist so that the rules can treat units with different jobs differently.

### Infantry

Infantry covers every unit that fights on foot, and is the bulk of most armies. It has four
sub-categories.

#### Regular Infantry

Foot soldiers of roughly human size: the dependable mainstay of an army, able to take almost any
formation and do almost any job.

**5 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +2 · Unit Mass 1 each.** Rules: Parry, Massed Infantry, Press of
Battle.

#### Heavy Infantry

Warriors bigger than a man but short of monstrous, and elite troops in heavy kit, often used as
bodyguards and sent to the thick of the fight.

**4 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +2 · Unit Mass 1 each.** Rules: Parry, Massed Infantry, Press of
Battle, Steady in the Ranks.

#### Monstrous Infantry

Brutes of roughly human shape that fight on foot but stand two or three times as tall as regular
or heavy infantry.

**3 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +2 · Unit Mass 3 each.** Rules: Clumsy.

#### Swarms

Seething masses of small vermin (rats, snakes, insects and the like), called up by magic or let
loose by handlers. A swarm unit is made of several bases. Each base carries many creatures but
counts as **one model**.

**No Rank Bonus · Unit Mass 3 each.** Rules: Undisciplined, Insignificant, Beneath Notice.

#### Infantry rules

**Press of Battle.** When a unit with this rule is in combat order, its fighting rank counts as two
ranks deep, except in a turn in which it charged. Any model directly behind a model in the real
fighting rank, and in base contact with it, counts as being in the fighting rank too (chapter 6).

**Massed Infantry.** When working out the Melee Outcome, a side with the higher Unit Mass
gains **+1** to its result if at least one of its units has this rule.

**Parry.** While in melee, a model with this rule that has a hand weapon and a shield, and chooses
to fight with them, improves its armour value by 1, to no better than 3+.

**Steady in the Ranks.** A Heavy Infantry unit in Close Order or Open Order only becomes Disrupted
by an enemy in its flank or rear if that enemy has a Unit Mass of **10 or more**, not the usual five.

**Clumsy.** Only a character who also has this rule may join a unit with it.

**Insignificant.** Lines of sight are drawn across a swarm as though it were not there. Swarms are
also ignored when working out whether an enemy character can be targeted.

**Beneath Notice.** A swarm never causes Panic in friendly units, whatever happens to it.

**Undisciplined.** Neither the General's Inspiring Presence nor the Battle Standard's Hold Your
Ground (chapter 8) works for a unit that has this rule.

### Cavalry

Cavalry means riders on war beasts, most often horses or something like them. It also covers packs
of beasts with no riders, which tend to fight more like cavalry than like foot troops. It has four
sub-categories.

#### Light Cavalry

Fast riders who range wide, scout, encircle the enemy and strike quickly.

**5 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +1 · Unit Mass 2 each.** Rules: Cavalry Support, Split Profile
(Cavalry).

#### Heavy Cavalry

Knights and other elite riders in heavy armour on powerful mounts, built for the shattering charge.

**4 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +1 · Unit Mass 2 each.** Rules: Cavalry Support, Split Profile
(Cavalry).

#### Monstrous Cavalry

Riders on huge, hard-to-tame beasts. Rare, and greatly feared.

**3 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +1 · Unit Mass 3 each.** Rules: Clumsy, Split Profile (Cavalry).

#### War Beasts

Hunting animals such as hounds and wolves: not clever, but quick over the ground and dangerous up
close.

**5 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +1 · Unit Mass 1 each.** Rules: Undisciplined (see Infantry
rules above).

#### Cavalry rules

**Split Profile (Cavalry).** Most cavalry models, though not all, are a rider and a mount played as
one model, with a row of the profile each. Then:

- A special rule that applies to the rider or the mount applies to both, unless stated otherwise.
- Rider and mount each use their **own** Combat Mastery, Marksmanship, Might, Reflexes and Strikes,
  and their own weapons.
- Enemy rolls to hit in melee are always made against the **rider's** Combat Mastery.
- Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks use the **mount's** Might.
- Armour Rolls use the **rider's** armour value.
- When the rider is reduced to 0 Hit Points, the whole model is removed.

**Cavalry Support.** A rider can lean over a comrade to strike, but a mount cannot. When a cavalry
model is able to make a supporting attack (chapter 6), only the rider attacks; the mount does not.

**Clumsy.** As under Infantry rules above.

### Chariots

Most chariots are wheeled war carts hauled by beasts and crewed by well-armed warriors. The
category also takes in other big objects that are pushed or carried into battle, such as mobile
shrines. It has two sub-categories.

#### Light Chariots

Quick, light chariots used as shooting platforms or as mounts for heroes. They often fight in
units and harry the enemy's flanks much as light cavalry do.

**3 per rank · Rank Bonus up to +1 · Unit Mass 3 each.** Rules: Firing Platform, Churning
Wheels, Iron Shod Wheels, Split Profile (Chariots).

#### Heavy Chariots

Strong, slow, armoured chariots that pick up speed and crash deep into the enemy's lines.

**No Rank Bonus · Unit Mass 5 each.** Rules: Firing Platform, Lumbering,
Scythed Wheels, Iron Shod Wheels, Split Profile (Chariots).

#### Chariot rules

**Split Profile (Chariots).** A chariot is the vehicle, its crew and, usually, the beasts that pull
it, all played as one model. Then:

- A special rule that applies to the chariot, the crew or the beasts applies to all three, unless
  stated otherwise.
- The model moves at the **beasts'** Movement or, if it has no beasts, at the chariot's own.
- Crew and beasts each use their **own** Combat Mastery, Marksmanship, Might, Reflexes and Strikes,
  and their own weapons.
- Enemy rolls to hit in melee are always made against the **crew's** Combat Mastery.
- Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks use the **chariot's** Might.
- When the chariot is reduced to 0 Hit Points, the whole model is removed.

**Iron Shod Wheels.** Chariots are made for open ground. This model treats every piece of difficult
terrain as dangerous terrain, and every linear obstacle as impassable. If it rolls a 1 on a
Dangerous Terrain test, it loses **D3** Hit Points instead of one (chapter 11).

**Churning Wheels.** Only a character who also has this rule may join a unit with it.

**Firing Platform.** Warriors in a chariot can shoot every way. Its vision arc is a full
**360°** whenever it shoots or casts a spell.

**Scythed Wheels.** A Heavy Chariot's Impact Hits carry **Pierce -2**.

**Lumbering.** A Lumbering model is not nimble:

- It counts as a **Close Order** formation and follows every rule for one, even when it is a
  single model on its own.
- After it moves, unless it charged, marched or fled, it may **pivot about its centre up to 90°**.
  (A single big model is still a little handier than a packed block of troops.)
- It can never join a unit, and no character can join it.

### Monsters

Monsters are so big and strong that they usually fight alone, not in units. Because of their size
they still count as **Close Order** units, not Skirmishers. There are two sub-categories.

#### Monstrous Creatures

Great beasts much larger than Monstrous Infantry or Monstrous Cavalry, though not quite big enough
to make the ground shake.

**No Rank Bonus · Unit Mass equal to starting Hit Points.** Rules: Ridden Monster, Lumbering.

#### Behemoths

The very largest things on the battlefield: towering beasts, giants, vast constructs of craft or
sorcery.

**No Rank Bonus · Unit Mass equal to starting Hit Points.** Rules: Ridden Monster, Lumbering,
Crushing Tread.

#### Monster rules

**Ridden Monster.** A character riding a monster (a Monstrous Creature or a Behemoth) makes a model
whose troop type is the **mount's** sub-category.

**Lumbering.** As under Chariot rules above.

**Crushing Tread.** Stomp Attacks made by a Behemoth have **Pierce -2**. This does not apply against
another monster (a Monstrous Creature or Behemoth): such targets are too big to be stamped on
properly.

### Characters

A model with "(Character)" after its troop type is a character. Characters are covered in
chapter 8.

### War Machines

War machines are cannons, bolt throwers and their like, able to tear apart a regiment, smash a wall
or bring down a monster with one good shot. The common types work as weapons and have their own
rules in chapter 10. Here are the rules every war machine has as a troop type.

**No Rank Bonus · Unit Mass equal to starting Hit Points.** Rules: Split Profile (War Machine),
Hired Crew, Weapon of War.

#### War machine rules

**Split Profile (War Machine).** A war machine is the engine and the crew who work it, played as
one model. Then:

- It is a single model. A special rule that applies to the machine or the crew applies to both,
  unless stated otherwise.
- **In melee**, use the **crew's** Resilience and Hit Points.
- **Out of melee**, use the **machine's** Resilience and Hit Points.
- The crew's Strikes go down by 1 for each Hit Point the crew has lost.
- Armour Rolls use the **crew's** armour value.
- When either the crew or the machine is reduced to 0 Hit Points, the whole model is removed.

**Hired Crew.** If a war machine Breaks and flees from a melee, its crew run off and leave it to
the enemy. The model counts as destroyed and is removed from play. A war machine that merely loses
a round of melee is not abandoned: it may still Give Ground as usual, and may still Fall Back in Good Order (chapter 7).

**Weapon of War.** Once set up, a war machine is heavy and slow:

- It cannot march, declare a charge or pursue.
- It takes **-1** on any Flee roll it makes (to a minimum of 1).
- It may pivot freely about its centre just before it shoots. Doing so does not count as moving.
- Follow-up moves are allowed, under the usual rules.

## Unusual formations

Each unit must take up a formation. The formations a unit may use are given by special rules named
after them (Close Order, Open Order, Skirmishers and so on). A unit with more than one of these
chooses its formation when it deploys, and can switch to another one during the game by reforming
(chapter 4).

Close Order, the standard block of ranks and files, is covered in chapter 4. This section covers the
two other common formations, **Open Order** and **Skirmish**. Every army can field units able to use
them, and they are well suited to working through terrain, harassing the enemy from odd angles and
guarding your own flanks.

### Open Order

An Open Order unit is laid out just like a Close Order one. The difference is in how it moves and
deals with terrain.

- It is two or more models touching, side against side with their front corners lined up, all
  facing the same way.
- They stand in one or more ranks (rows across) and a number of files (rows front to back).
- Every rank holds the same number of models where possible. When that can't be done, the short
  rank is the back one.

#### Unit shape

As with Close Order, the shape of an Open Order unit matters:

- **Combat Order.** Its ranks hold more models than its files do (wider than deep), or the two
  match (square). In Combat Order the unit may claim a **Rank Bonus** of +1 per rank behind
  the first, up to its troop type's maximum (higher if a special rule allows). An incomplete back
  rank still counts if it holds enough models.
- **Marching Column.** Its files hold more models than its ranks do (deeper than wide). A
  Marching Column claims no Rank Bonus and cannot charge, but **triples** its Movement when it
  marches.

#### Highly manoeuvrable

Open Order troops fight as individuals rather than shoulder to shoulder, so they are much handier
than a Close Order block. A unit in Open Order has both of these abilities:

- **Quick Turn.** In the Remaining Moves sub-phase, after it moves, the unit may pivot about its
  centre up to **90°**, unless it marched.
- **Dispersed Ranks.** Difficult and dangerous terrain never make the unit Disrupted.

### Skirmish formation

A unit in Skirmish formation (**Skirmishers** for short) has no ranks or files. It moves as one
loose group or ragged line, quick on its feet and good at using cover.

#### Spacing and coherency

- Skirmishers are not placed in base contact. Instead the unit forms one loose, unbroken group, with
  **each model no more than 1" from some other model** of the unit.
- The unit must end every move in that state: this is its **coherency**.
- When you remove casualties, you may not take a model whose removal would break coherency; take
  another model instead.
- If a Skirmish unit ever loses coherency anyway, remove models as casualties until one coherent
  group is left. Those models have deserted the field.

#### Facing and Line of Sight

Skirmishers have no flank or rear arcs: every direction is their front, so they have a **360°**
vision arc. Charges and shots may go any way at all, free of the usual 90° arcs.

Other units can see through a Skirmish unit wherever a line can be drawn between its models. The
models themselves block Line of Sight as normal.

#### Moving

- Each Skirmisher moves on its own. The unit never turns or wheels and makes no other manoeuvres,
  though it may reform into another formation if it can.
- Each model may go in any direction with no penalty, as long as the unit keeps coherency.
- No other unit may move through the gaps between Skirmishers. Fleeing units may flee through them,
  as through any other unit.
- Skirmishers that arrive as reinforcements are set up with every model within 1" of the
  battlefield edge.

#### Shooting

Skirmishers fire in any direction. Their own models still block Line of Sight, including for other
models in the same unit.

**Shooting at Skirmishers.** A unit shooting at Skirmishers takes **-1 to hit**, provided every model
in the target unit has a Unit Mass of 1.

#### Charging with Skirmishers

A Skirmish unit may charge an enemy unit that more than half (50%) of its models can see when
charges are declared. Which arc it charges into is worked out in the usual way (chapter 4).

1. Move the Skirmisher **closest to the target** first.
2. Then move the rest one at a time, each as directly as it can towards the target, to touch it
   and build a fighting rank.
3. Models that can't reach base contact line up behind the fighting rank.
4. If any models can't line up, the unit has lost coherency (see above).

#### Charging Skirmishers with a formed unit

A formed unit of any kind (Close Order, Open Order and so on) charging Skirmishers must head as
directly as it can for the **nearest visible model** of the target. It may wheel once, at any point
of the move, as usual.

On contact the charger does **not** wheel to line up with the target. The Skirmishers form against the
charger's fighting rank, but no Skirmisher may move further than its own Movement to do so. Those
that can't reach base contact line up behind the fighting rank.

#### Skirmishers charging Skirmishers

1. Move the charging model **closest to the target** first, by the shortest route to the target.
2. Move the rest of the charging unit as directly as possible to form a fighting rank with that
   first model, keeping it as near the middle of the rank as you can.
3. Charging models without enough movement to join the fighting rank line up behind it.
4. The target unit's models then line up against the charger's fighting rank, none moving further
   than its own Movement. Those that can't reach base contact line up behind the fighting rank.

#### Skirmishers in melee

Once in melee, Skirmishers close up shoulder to shoulder, bases touching and front corners level,
against the enemy to make a fighting rank. Whether they charged or were charged,
they always do this on contact. Any that can't reach the enemy line up in ranks behind the fighting
rank.

They stay closed up until the fight is over: a Melee Phase ends with no enemy still engaging
them. Then they spread out again, moving as little as possible, back into
Skirmish formation.

- **No Rank Bonus.** A unit that was in Skirmish formation when it became engaged cannot claim a
  Rank Bonus, even though it has closed up.
- **No Disruption.** Skirmishers engaging an enemy's flank or rear do not make that enemy Disrupted.

#### Skirmishers and Panic

- Skirmishers cause Panic in friendly units as usual if they are wiped out, or if they Break and
  flee.
- Fleeing Skirmishers that pass through friendly Skirmishers cause Panic in them as usual.
- Fleeing Skirmishers that pass through a **formed** friendly unit (Close Order, Open Order and so
  on) do not cause Panic in it.

Panic is explained in chapter 7.

#### Characters and Skirmishers

A character may join a Skirmish unit only if the character has the **same troop sub-category** as
the unit. A Light Cavalry character can join Light Cavalry Skirmishers, for example, but not Heavy
Cavalry Skirmishers. This overrides the usual rules for characters joining units (chapter 8).
