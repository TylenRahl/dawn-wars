# 3. The Turn

## Rounds and turns

A game is played in **rounds**. In each round both players take one full **turn**, one after
the other, so when the game ends you have both had the same number of turns.

- A rule that says "turn" means one player's turn only. A rule that covers both players' turns
  says "round".
- The player whose turn it is is the **active player**. The other is the **inactive player**.
- **First turn.** The battle you are playing says who goes first. Most often you roll off for
  it, but some battles decide it another way.
- **Game length.** A game normally lasts six rounds. Some end after five, some run to seven,
  and in some battles the length is not fixed.

## The turn sequence

Your turn has four **phases**, always played in this order. Each phase is split into four
steps, called **sub-phases**. Finish one sub-phase before you start the next. Within a
sub-phase you may usually act with your units in any order you like.

1. **Strategy Phase.** You cast Enchantment and Hex spells, use special rules that act now,
   then try to rally your fleeing units. Covered in full below.
2. **Manoeuvre Phase.** First you declare charges, and the enemy declares how each charged
   unit reacts. Charging units move next, then compulsory moves are made (fleeing units, for
   example). Last, your other units move, and Wizards may cast Conveyance spells. See
   chapter 4.
3. **Fire Phase.** Your units with missile weapons shoot, your war machines fire, and Wizards
   may cast Magic Missile and Magical Vortex spells. See chapter 5, and chapter 10 for war
   machines.
4. **Melee Phase.** Units in base contact with the enemy fight hand to hand, and Wizards in
   the fight may cast Assailment spells. A unit that loses may be pushed back, or break and
   flee. See chapter 6, and chapter 7 for breaking, fleeing and pursuit. When every combat
   has been resolved, your turn is over.

Spells and casting are covered in chapter 9.

## The Strategy Phase

In the Strategy Phase you set up the turn ahead: your characters give orders, your Wizards
work their first spells, and your leaders try to bring fleeing troops back into line. It has
four sub-phases:

1. **Start of turn**
2. **Command**
3. **Conjuration**
4. **Rally fleeing units**

### 1. Start of turn

Anything that a rule says happens "at the start of the turn" is done now.

- A few units must take a test or carry out an action at the start of each turn. Their own
  rules say exactly what. Resolve them in any order you choose.
- The battle you are playing may also ask for a test now, or for a check on whether someone
  has met a victory condition before play carries on.

This is also the natural pause between turns. Clear away dead models and stray dice, and ask
your opponent anything you need to know about their army, such as how one of their special
rules or magic items works. Answer such questions freely when it is your opponent's turn.

### 2. Command

Some models, usually characters, have special rules meant for the Command sub-phase:
inspiring orders, powers from magic items, and the like.

1. Pick one of your models that has such a rule and is not fleeing.
2. Say which rule it will use, if any, and which unit or units it affects, if that matters.
3. Make any test the rule calls for.
4. Repeat for every other model in your army with a Command rule.

Unless its rule says otherwise, a model may use each such special rule only once per Command
sub-phase.

### 3. Conjuration

Now your Wizards may cast their **Enchantment** and **Hex** spells.

1. Pick one of your Wizards that is not fleeing.
2. If it knows any Enchantment or Hex spells, you may try to cast them.
3. Each time one is cast, your opponent may try to dispel it.
4. Repeat for each of your other Wizards.

The two kinds of spell:

- **Enchantment** spells help your own side. If one needs a target, it must be a friendly
  unit. They mostly affect friendly units, though now and then one also affects the enemy.
- **Hex** spells harm the enemy. If one needs a target, it must be an enemy unit. They mostly
  affect enemy units, though now and then one also affects your own.
- Casting one particular Enchantment or Hex spell on a unit a second time in the same turn
  does nothing more.
- Bonuses and penalties from **different** Enchantment and Hex spells add together, but they
  can never raise a characteristic above 10 or drop it below 1.

How to cast and dispel is covered in chapter 9.

### 4. Rally fleeing units

You must try to rally each of your units that is fleeing.

1. Pick one of your fleeing units and make a **Rally test** for it. This is a Resolve Test:
   roll 2D6, and the test is passed if the score is no higher than the unit's Resolve. A unit
   whose models have different Resolve uses the highest. A natural double 1 always passes and
   a natural double 6 always fails (chapter 2).
2. **Fail:** the unit has not rallied, and keeps fleeing.
3. **Pass:** the unit has rallied.
4. Pick another fleeing unit and repeat, until each fleeing unit has had its test.

#### Insurmountable losses

A unit that has lost many of its models finds it harder to come back:

| Unit is down to | Effect on its Rally test |
|---|---|
| under half (50%) of its starting models | -1 to its Resolve |
| under a quarter (25%) of its starting models | it passes **only** on a natural double 1 |

#### Rallied units

A unit that rallies is back in the fight.

- It may make a free reform at once (chapter 4).
- It may not charge this turn.
- It counts as having moved, for the purpose of shooting.
- Apart from that, it acts as normal.

#### Units that fail to rally

A unit that fails its Rally test goes on fleeing. It makes its flee move in the Compulsory
Moves sub-phase of the Manoeuvre Phase (chapters 4 and 7).
