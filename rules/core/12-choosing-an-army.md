# 12. Choosing an Army

Before a battle each player builds an army from their army list and writes it down. This chapter
covers how: the points limit, the composition list that shapes the army, and the extra forces you may
bring along.

## Points and game size

Every model and upgrade costs **points**. Before the game, agree a points limit with your opponent.
Each army must come as close to that total as it can **without going over**. In a 3,000 point game,
both armies aim for 3,000 points and neither may pass it.

Bigger limits mean bigger, longer games. At 2,000 points, players who know the rules can expect a
game of about two hours. Smaller games are quicker; very large ones can fill most of a day. Try
several sizes and settle on the one you like.

## Army lists

Each faction has an **army list** with the profiles and rules of all its models. It is split into up
to six parts, though not every faction uses all of them (some have no chariots, some no war
machines):

1. Characters
2. Infantry
3. Cavalry
4. Chariots
5. Monsters
6. War Machines

Some army lists offer **heroes with a name of their own**, each with set gear and rules. Every
one is a single person, so your army may include each named hero once at most.

## Composition lists

The army list says what a unit *is*. The **composition list** says what role it plays in your army,
and so how many of it you may take. There are two kinds:

- **Grand Host.** Every faction's standard composition list. It offers the widest choice of units and
  gives a broad, balanced army.
- **Themed Host.** Extra composition lists with a strong flavour. A Themed Host usually drops some of
  the Grand Host's units and leans on one type of unit, or on the troops of one region. It makes a
  more specialised army at the cost of some flexibility.

### Categories

A composition list sorts its units into categories:

| Category | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Characters** | The army's leaders: heroes, champions of battle and Wizards. |
| **Core** | The bulk of the army, its everyday troops. |
| **Special** | Veterans and skilled troops that can hold a line or do great deeds alone. |
| **Rare** | The strangest and strongest: great monsters, odd war machines, elite soldiers. |

Many lists add one or both of these:

| Category | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Mercenaries** | Named units hired from other army lists (see Mercenaries). |
| **Allies** | Armies from which you may draw one allied contingent (see Allied contingents). |

### Percentages

Each category carries a percentage of your points limit, which you **may** spend on it ("up to") or
**must** spend on it ("at least"). The Grand Host percentages are much the same for every faction:

| Category | Points |
|---|---|
| Characters | 50% at most |
| Core | 25% or more |
| Special | 50% at most |
| Rare | 25% at most |
| Mercenaries | 20% at most |
| Allies | 25% at most, all on one allied contingent |

*Example:* in a 2,000 point army, Core units must cost at least 500 points (25%), and Special units
may cost no more than 1,000 points (50%).

### Unit limits

A number next to a unit's name in the composition list limits how many units of it you may take:

| Shown as | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0-2** (a range) | Between none and two units of this kind. |
| **1+** (a number and a plus) | At least one unit of this kind; more are allowed. |
| **up to 3 per 1,000 points** | No more than three such units for each **full** 1,000 points of the points limit. |
| nothing | No lower or upper limit. |

### At least three units

Every army needs at least **three units that are not characters**. Units of swarms, war beasts or war
machines do not count towards the three.

## The General

Every army must include at least one character, to be its General. If you have more than one, the
General is the one chosen by the rules in chapter 8: the highest Resolve, your pick among a tie, and
never a Loner.

## Mercenaries

Some composition lists have a mercenaries section, naming units from other army lists that you may
hire into your army. Every unit hired this way gains the **Mercenaries** special rule:

- It can't use the General's Inspiring Presence or a Battle Standard's Hold Your Ground (chapter 8).
- No character from another army list may join it.

### Unreliable hirelings

Before deployment, roll a D6 for each mercenary unit in your army.

- **2+:** the unit turns up as agreed. Treat it as part of your army, as if it came from your own
  army list.
- **1:** something has gone wrong. Roll on the table below at once.

| D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Hung over.** The unit gains the Stupidity special rule (chapter 13). If it already has Stupidity, add +1 to the dice every time it takes a Stupidity test. |
| 2-3 | **Underpaid.** For the whole battle the unit has -1 Movement and -1 Reflexes, neither going below 1. |
| 4-5 | **Late and lost.** The unit gains the Ambushers special rule (chapter 13) and must start the battle in reserve. If it already had Ambushers, it arrives in turn 2 only on a natural 6, not the usual 4+. |
| 6 | **Not their fight.** For the whole battle the unit has -1 Resolve, never going below 2. |

## Allied contingents

Most composition lists have an allies section naming the armies from which you may draw an **allied
contingent**: a small army of a friendly power that fights beside your main army.

### Building a contingent

An allied contingent is a small army in its own right, built from the composition list of its own
faction and following that list's rules and percentages.

- **Points.** Its total cost must fit inside the allies percentage of your **main** army's
  composition list. In a 2,000 point army with allies at up to 25%, the contingent may cost at most
  500 points.
- **General.** It must include at least one character as its own General.
- **Units.** It needs at least one unit that is not a character. There is no upper limit.

### Rules for every contingent

- Characters and units of an allied contingent may join only each other: none of its characters
  may join a main army unit, and no main army character may join one of its units.
- Inspiring Presence and Hold Your Ground (chapter 8) never cross over. The contingent can't use the
  main army's General or Battle Standard, and the main army can't use the contingent's.
- Army special rules stay with their own side. One that affects the main army has no effect on the
  contingent, and the reverse.
- For everything else (Panic tests, spells, Melee Outcome and so on) the contingent's units and
  characters count as part of the main army.

### Types of alliance

Most contingents are **trusted allies** and use only the rules above. Some composition lists mark an
alliance as **Uneasy**, or even **Suspicious**, for armies that rarely get on.

**Uneasy.** Everything above, and also:

- To every main army unit, each unit of the contingent counts as having the **Levies** special rule:
  it can't use Inspiring Presence or Hold Your Ground, and a unit without Levies need not take a
  Panic test when a friendly Levies unit breaks and flees from melee.
- Main army Wizards don't treat the contingent's units as friendly when choosing targets for spells,
  and the contingent's Wizards don't treat main army units as friendly either.

**Suspicious.** Everything for Uneasy, and also:

- A contingent unit that flees through a main army unit, or a main army unit that flees through a
  contingent unit, takes Peril tests just as if it had fled through the enemy (chapter 7).
- A contingent unit within 6" of a main army unit has -1 Resolve, and so does a main army unit within
  6" of a contingent unit.

## Regimental units and detachments

Some well-drilled armies field big units guarded by smaller ones at their sides. In those army lists,
a unit with the **Regimental Unit** special rule may bring up to **two** smaller units with the
**Detachment** special rule.

A model with both special rules can form a unit that is either a regimental unit or a detachment,
but not both at once. Say which on your muster list, and make it clear to your opponent at
deployment.

### Building them

**The regimental unit.** Build it as normal on your muster list and note down which detachments
go with it, two at most.

**Each detachment:**

- has at least **5** models and at most **half** as many as its regimental unit, rounding down,
- may buy its listed optional upgrades as normal,
- may have **no** command group and no command models,
- must be assigned to exactly one regimental unit, noted on the muster list.

A detachment is an ordinary unit in every way except for the rules below. Spells, magic items and
special rules that affect its regimental unit do not affect it, unless they say so.

### Detachment rules

**Deploying together.** Deploy each detachment at the same time as its regimental unit and within
**3"** of it. If the regimental unit is kept in reserve as reinforcements, its detachments are too,
and they arrive with it.

**Borrowed Resolve.** A detachment within 3" of its regimental unit may borrow that unit's Resolve,
as long as the regimental unit is not fleeing. If its own is higher it may keep its own.

**Shared temper.** While within 3" of its regimental unit, a detachment gains any of these special
rules that the regimental unit has: Stubborn, Frenzy, Immune to Psychology and Hatred (X).

**Supporting actions.** When a regimental unit is declared as the target of an enemy charge and does
not Flee as its charge reaction, each of its detachments may declare a supporting action, provided
the detachment is within 3" of it and is neither in combat nor fleeing. A detachment may try only
one supporting action each turn:

- **Supporting Fire.** A detachment with missile weapons at once tries a Stand & Shoot charge
  reaction (chapter 4), as though it were the one being charged. If it can't (because the enemy is
  too close, say), it does nothing and may not switch to another supporting action.
- **Supporting Charge.** When the Charge Moves sub-phase ends, if the detachment is still neither
  in combat nor fleeing, it charges out of sequence. Its target must be one enemy unit that charged
  its regimental unit this turn.

## The muster list

Your **muster list** is the written record of your army. For each unit, give:

- how many models it starts the battle with,
- their equipment,
- every optional upgrade: equipment, command models, magic items and the like,
- its points cost.

Add up the total for the army, to show it fits the agreed limit. Swap muster lists with your
opponent before deployment.

You may add more if you like: profiles, special rules, spell and magic item texts. None of it is
required, but it saves looking things up during play.
