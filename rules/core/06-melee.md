# 6. The Melee Phase

The Melee Phase is where units locked together trade blows. Every melee on the table is fought
in this phase, and no unit in base contact with the enemy may sit one out. Both players take
part, but the active player decides the order in which the melees are fought.

## The phase sequence

A **melee** is any group of units, from both sides, that touch one another. Units in base
contact with at least one enemy unit are **engaged**. Each melee is fought on its own, from
start to finish, as one **round**. The active player picks a melee and both players run it
through four steps:

1. **Fight.** Models strike in order of Reflexes, highest first. Hits are rolled, wounds are
   rolled, Armour Rolls are taken and casualties come off. Then the next Reflexes value strikes,
   and so on until every model that can fight has fought.
2. **Melee Outcome.** Each side adds up its points. The side with more wins, by the
   difference: 1, 2 or more points. Equal scores are a draw.
3. **Break Test.** Each losing unit takes a Break Test. It may break and flee, fall
   back in good order, or only give ground (chapter 7).
4. **Follow up and pursue.** Winning units may follow up, pursue or hold their place
   (chapter 7).

When those four steps are done, the active player picks the next melee. Repeat until none are
left.

The Fight step itself runs in four parts, repeated for each Reflexes value:

1. Choose the melee and see which models can fight.
2. Rolls to hit.
3. Rolls to wound, then the defender's Armour Rolls.
4. Remove casualties.

Once casualties are off, look for models with a lower Reflexes that have still to strike. If
there are any, run parts 2 to 4 again for them. When nobody is left to strike, go on to the
Melee Outcome.

## 1. Who can fight

Most of a unit never reaches the enemy. Only the models at the point of contact fight; the rest
wait behind to fill gaps.

### Base contact

A model touching an enemy model may always fight. It does not matter which side of either base
is touching, and a corner touching a corner is enough.

### The fighting rank

Any line of models, whether a rank or a file, with even one model touching the enemy counts
as a **fighting rank**. Every model in a fighting rank may fight if the enemy unit is
within a number of inches equal to that model's Movement.

### Supporting attacks

Some weapons let a model make **supporting attacks**. Such a model may strike from the line
directly behind its unit's fighting rank, as long as the enemy unit is within its Movement in
inches. Supporting attacks are never made into the flank or rear, and a model already standing in
a fighting rank can't make them.

### Models that miss their turn

- **Slain first.** A model in the fighting rank that is killed by an enemy with a higher
  Reflexes, before it has struck, loses its Strikes.
- **Stepped forward.** A model that moved up into the fighting rank during this phase to fill a
  gap can't strike in this phase, whatever its Reflexes.

### Incomplete fighting ranks

A unit fighting to its flank or rear, or one with a character on its edge that does not fit its
ranks, may have a short fighting rank. Treat the fighting rank as holding as many models as the
biggest rank or file behind it; warriors from the middle press up to fill it.

## 2. Who strikes first

Models strike in order of **Reflexes (Rf)**. Count down from the highest value present in the
melee to the lowest. When a model's value comes up, it makes all its attacks.

### Charging

A unit that charged this turn adds to the Reflexes of its models in that turn's Melee Phase. It
gets **+1 Rf for each full inch it moved before it made contact**, up to:

| Charged into the enemy's | Most it can add |
|---|---|
| Front arc | +3 Rf |
| Flank or rear arc | +4 Rf |

No model's Reflexes can go above 10 this way.

A **disordered charge** gets no Reflexes bonus at all. Charges become disordered through terrain
(see Terrain below) and some other rules.

### Simultaneous blows

Models on both sides with the same Reflexes (after modifiers) strike at the same moment. To
keep it simple, the active player rolls their attacks first, then the other player rolls. The
casualties the active player causes still get to strike back, since they struck at the same
time.

### Split profiles

A model with Strikes at more than one Reflexes value makes each set when its value comes up. If
it dies first, the sets it has not yet made are lost. It can also lose attacks if every enemy it
could have hit is already dead.

## 3. How many Strikes

- A model **in base contact** with an enemy model makes as many attacks as its **Strikes (St)**.
- A model that can fight but is **not** in base contact makes **one** attack, whatever its
  Strikes.

A model with Combat Mastery 0 misses with every attack, and every attack against it hits
without a roll. A model with 0 Strikes makes no attacks at all.

### Wizards in melee

A Wizard who is engaged and knows Assailment spells may try to cast them when its Reflexes value
comes up, as well as making its normal attacks (chapter 9). Hits from an Assailment spell that
uses a template land on the enemy's rear ranks, so they never thin out the fighting rank.

## 4. Choosing targets

### Dividing attacks

A model touching more than one enemy model picks which to strike. A model with more than 1 Strike
may share its attacks among them. Say how the attacks are shared before you roll to hit.

### More than one enemy unit

When a fighting rank touches more than one enemy unit, each model in it must strike an enemy
model it touches, if it can. A model touching no enemy strikes the closest enemy unit; between
two that are equally close, its owner chooses.

### Fighting on several sides

A unit engaged in more than one of its arcs has more than one fighting rank. Engaged to the
front and one flank, for example, its front rank is a fighting rank and so is the file along
that flank.

A model that belongs to more than one fighting rank must strike an enemy it touches, if any,
picked by its owner. If it touches no enemy, it strikes the nearest enemy unit, with its owner
picking between equally near units.

### Batches of dice

Roll all of a unit's attacks at once when you can: count the models fighting and the Strikes of
each. When models differ in Combat Mastery or Might, carry weapons with different profiles, or
are striking targets that differ, the numbers needed will differ too. Roll each group as its own
batch, and tell your opponent what each batch is, where it is aimed and what it needs. Keep the
same batches when you roll to wound.

## 6. Roll to wound

Pick up the dice that hit and roll them again. Find the Might of the weapon (from its profile)
down the side and the target's Resilience along the top. A die that scores the number shown or

A **natural 1 never wounds**, whatever the modifiers.

## 7. Armour Rolls

A model's armour value comes from what it wears and carries, worked out as in chapter 5. Light
armour gives 6+; a shield makes it one better, so light armour and a shield give 5+. Gear can
improve armour no further than 2+. A model with no armour at all counts as having 7+ when a
rule modifies or improves its armour.

For each wound, the defender rolls a D6 for the wounded model:

- **Equal to or above** its armour value: the armour holds and the wound is ignored.
- **Below** it: the wound is **unsaved**.

A **natural 1 always fails** any kind of Armour Roll, whatever the modifiers.

## 8. Remove casualties

Each unsaved wound takes 1 Hit Point off the target unit. A model at 0 Hit Points is a casualty
and is removed. Keep going until every unsaved wound has been used or the unit has no models
left.

### Who actually falls

The blows land on the fighting rank, but you take the casualties from the **back rank** as
usual. Think of the fallen as coming off the ends of the fighting rank: the men behind step up
to fill the gaps, and the survivors in front close in on the foe. Models that stepped up can't
strike this phase (see Who can fight), but the ones that closed in are now more likely to be in
base contact.

Put casualties beside their unit rather than back in the box until the melee is over. That shows
at a glance how many Hit Points were lost, for the Melee Outcome, and how many models are gone
from the fighting rank and can't strike back.

### Excess casualties

A unit can lose more models than its fighting rank holds. Remove them as normal: the extra deaths
fall in the line behind. If that unit can make supporting attacks, each extra casualty takes one
supporting attack away.

### Shrinking units

When casualties leave two engaged units no longer touching, slide them back into base contact by
the least distance you can. This move can't change facing, tidy the ranks or bring in another
unit.

## 9. Terrain in melee

Before the battle, agree with your opponent which kind each terrain piece is (chapter 11).

| Terrain | Effect in melee |
|---|---|
| Open ground and hills | No effect, but a unit fighting from higher ground can claim the high ground bonus (below). |
| Difficult terrain | If, as the Melee Phase begins, at least a quarter (25%) of a unit's models are in it, the unit becomes **Disrupted** and can't claim a Rank Bonus. |
| Dangerous terrain, woods | Count as difficult terrain in melee. |
| Impassable terrain | If it stops a charger from aligning to the enemy, the charge is **disordered**. If, once contact is made, the units still can't be aligned and a gap is left, the charging unit is **Disrupted**. |
| Decorations under 2" across (a signpost, a well) | Ignore them; units fight around them. If one gets in the way of placing models, note where it stood, lift it off and put it back later. |

### Obstacles

- **Low obstacles** (under 2" high: walls, hedges). A unit in a melee that ends up straddling one
  is **Disrupted**.
- **High obstacles** (2" high or more) count as impassable terrain in melee.

### Defending an obstacle

A unit behind a low obstacle defends it by moving its front rank up to touch it. The enemy may
charge the defenders as normal without crossing: the charger's front rank moves up to touch the
other side of the obstacle. A unit that charges defenders behind an obstacle makes a disordered
charge. Units with Fly (X) (chapter 13) are the exception: their charge stays orderly.

## 10. The Melee Outcome

When every model in the melee has struck, work out who won the round. If one side has been wiped
out, the other side wins, whatever the scores.

Otherwise each side adds up its points:

| Source | Points |
|---|---|
| Unsaved wounds caused | 1 per Hit Point lost by the enemy |
| Rank Bonus | +1 per rank |
| Standard | +1 |
| Battle Standard | +1 |
| Flank attack | +1 |
| Rear attack | +2 |
| High ground | +1 |
| Overkill | +1 per excess wound |
| Massed Infantry | +1 if your side has the higher Unit Mass |
| Other bonuses | As the rule says |

### Unsaved wounds

Count every Hit Point the enemy side lost this turn in the Melee Phase, plus every Hit Point
your unit took off them with a Stand and Shoot charge reaction this turn (chapter 4). Each is
worth 1 point. Count Hit Points, not dead models, since many models have more than one.

A model that a rule or attack removes outright counts as losing all the Hit Points it had left
at that moment.

### Rank Bonus

A unit in a formation wide enough for it claims **+1 for each rank behind the first**. The
models a rank needs to count, and the highest Rank Bonus allowed, depend on the troop type
(chapter 2). A rear rank that is not full still counts if it has enough models in it. A
**Disrupted** unit claims no Rank Bonus.

### Standards

- A unit with a standard bearer claims +1 (chapter 8).
- A unit with the Battle Standard claims a further +1, on top of any ordinary standard
  (chapter 8).

### Flank and rear

- Engaging an enemy unit in its flank arc: +1.
- Engaging an enemy unit in its rear arc: +2.

The two add together.

### High ground

If your unit's fighting rank stands higher than the enemy's, on the crest of a hill for example,
claim +1.

### Overkill

When a character in a challenge kills its foe and causes more unsaved wounds than the foe had Hit
Points left, claim +1 for each wound over, up to **+5**. Challenges are in chapter 8.

### Other bonuses

Many special rules add points. A Close Order unit in Combat Order, for instance, claims +1
(chapter 1). Each such rule gives its own conditions and how much it is worth.

### Several units on one side

Melees often hold more than two units. Then each side counts as follows:

- **Rank Bonus.** Don't add them up. Take only the single best Rank Bonus on your side. A unit
  with +1 and another with +2 give your side +2.
- **Standards.** Claim one standard's bonus however many of your units carry one. If any of them
  gives extra points (many magic standards do), you choose which one counts. The Battle Standard
  counts as normal; an army seldom has more than one.
- **Flank and rear.** Each bonus counts once per enemy unit. Two of your units on the two flanks
  of one enemy unit earn one flank bonus. One on its flank and one on its rear earn both bonuses.
- **High ground.** Only one unit in the whole melee can claim it: the one whose fighting rank
  stands highest. If two opposing units tie for highest, they cancel out and nobody claims it.
- **Other bonuses** always count for every unit that earns them, unless the rule says otherwise.
  Two Close Order units in Combat Order in one melee give their side +2.

### Winning and drawing

The side with the higher total wins the round. Every unit on the losing side then takes a Break
Test (chapter 7).

If the totals are **equal**, the melee is a **draw**. Nobody takes a Break Test. The units stay
locked where they are and fight another round in the next player's turn.
