# 8. Characters and Command Groups

Two kinds of model stand out from the ranks. **Characters** are heroes, leaders and Wizards: single
models with better characteristics, more options and access to magic items. **Command groups** are
the champion, standard bearer and musician bought as upgrades inside an ordinary unit.

# Part one: Characters

## Character models

A character is a unit of its own. It can fight alone, or it can join a friendly unit and fight as
part of it. You can tell a character from its profile: the word **(Character)** follows its troop
type in brackets.

Each army list names its characters in its own way, but they all use the rules in this chapter.

### Command range

Every character has a **Command range**, measured in inches from the character model. Many special
rules work only within it.

- A normal character's Command range equals its Resolve: Rs 7 gives a 7" range.
- The **General** and the **Battle Standard Bearer** always have a 12" range, whatever their Resolve.
- If either of them is a Large Target, or rides a mount with that special rule, the range grows to **18"**.

## The General

Every army has one General: the character who commands it and stands for you on the table.

**Choosing the General.** Your General is your character with the highest Resolve. If two or more
share the top score, pick one of them when you write your muster list (chapter 12). Tell your
opponent which model it is before you deploy. A character with the Loner special rule (chapter 13)
may never be the General.

**Inspiring Presence.** While your General is not fleeing, any friendly unit within the General's
Command range may use the General's Resolve in place of its own. A fleeing General gives nothing,
so rallying the General is often your first job.

## The Battle Standard

Many armies may give one character the **Battle Standard**: the army's great banner, a rallying
point for the whole force. It is bought as an option in the army list.

- A character carries it. Unless the army list says otherwise, the Battle Standard Bearer may not
  also be the General.
- If the bearer dies, the Battle Standard dies with them. No other model can take it up, not even one
  in the same unit. (Ordinary standards are different; see Standard bearers below.)

**Melee bonus.** A Battle Standard is worth **+1** to its side's Melee Outcome (chapter 6). It counts
even when the unit also has an ordinary standard. If somehow one side has two Battle Standards in the
same combat, only one of them counts.

**Hold Your Ground.** While the Battle Standard Bearer is not fleeing, friendly units within its
Command range may:

- re-roll a failed Panic test or a failed Rally test (chapter 7), and
- re-roll the 2D6 of a Break Test.

The second roll stands, even if it is worse.

## Troop type and formation

A character has a troop type like any model (chapter 2). Most are on foot, some sub-category of
infantry. A few huge characters are monsters in their own right. Many can buy a mount, and the mount
then sets the troop type, as below.

Characters seldom have formation rules of their own. Instead:

| The character is... | Its formation |
|---|---|
| part of a unit it has joined | whatever that unit uses |
| a Lone character (see below) | Skirmish, always |
| riding a ridden monster or a chariot | the formation of the mount |

## Mounted characters

A character may ride. Most mounts are simple beasts such as a horse, a wolf or a boar. Some army
lists also let a character take a monster or a chariot from elsewhere in the list.

### Cavalry mounts

On a light, heavy or monstrous cavalry mount the character takes the mount's sub-category of troop
type. It has a split profile like any rider (chapter 2), and it moves at the mount's Movement.

Some big cavalry mounts raise the rider's Resilience, Hit Points or both. The mount's profile says
so where this applies.

### Ridden monsters

On a behemoth or a monstrous creature, the whole model takes the monster's sub-category of troop
type. Its split profile works like this:

- A special rule of the rider also applies to the mount, and the other way round, unless stated
  otherwise.
- The model moves at the mount's Movement.
- Rider and mount each fight with their own CM, Mk, Mi, Rf and St, and their own weapons.
- Enemies in melee roll to hit against the **rider's** CM.
- Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks use the **mount's** Mi.
- For its Armour Roll the model uses the better armour value of the two.
- When the **rider** reaches zero Hit Points, the whole model is removed.

A ridden monster always raises the rider's Resilience, Hit Points or both; the mount's profile gives
the new values.

### Chariot mounts

In a light or heavy chariot the whole model takes the chariot's sub-category of troop type. The
character joins the crew, and the split profile works like this:

- A special rule of one part (chariot, character, crew or beasts) applies to all the others, unless
  stated otherwise.
- The model moves at the Movement of the beasts that pull it. With no beasts, use the chariot's own.
- Character, crew and beasts each fight with their own CM, Mk, Mi, Rf and St, and their own weapons.
- Enemies in melee roll to hit against the **character's** CM.
- Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks use the **chariot's** Mi.
- Enemies roll to wound against the higher Resilience: the chariot's or the character's.
- For its Armour Roll the model uses the better armour value: the chariot's or the character's.
- When the **chariot** reaches zero Hit Points, the whole model is removed.

**Extra Hit Points.** Add the character's HP to the chariot's. A character with 3 HP in a chariot
with 4 HP makes a model with 7 HP.

## Lone characters

A character on foot or on a cavalry mount (troop type infantry or cavalry) who has not joined a unit
is a **Lone character**. The moment they join a unit, they stop being one. A Lone character is always
in Skirmish formation and has two extra rules.

**Evade.** Suppose an enemy unit's pursuit move would reach a Lone character who is neither fleeing
nor in combat. Before the enemy moves, the character may try a Reflexes test. On a pass, move the
character up to their Movement in inches, and they must not end any nearer to any enemy unit than
they began. Then finish the enemy's pursuit move.

**Hard to pick out.** Enemies may not target a Lone character with shooting or with spells while the
character has, within **3"**, a friendly unit that is not fleeing and holds five or more models
sharing the character's troop type (infantry with infantry, cavalry with cavalry). This protection ends when
the character is the closest target.

## Characters in units

### Joining a unit

Most characters may join a friendly unit, unless a special rule or their troop type forbids it.
There are two ways in:

1. **At deployment:** set the character up as part of the unit.
2. **In the Remaining Moves sub-phase:** move the character into base contact with a friendly unit
   that is neither fleeing nor engaged in combat.

A unit joined during the Remaining Moves sub-phase may not move any more that phase. It counts as
having moved only if it moved before the character arrived.

A unit may hold several characters. Two characters may not join each other to make a unit of their
own.

### Where the character stands

- A joining character goes into the **front rank**. Push rank and file models (never command group
  models) back to the rear rank to make room.
- If the character's base won't fit in the ranks, stand it at one end of the front rank, touching it,
  beside the unit's flank.
- If there is no space in the front rank at all, the character goes in the rear rank.
- When the unit turns or reforms, once its move is over put every character who was in or beside the
  old front rank in or beside the new one.

### Leaving a unit

In the Remaining Moves sub-phase a character may simply walk away from the unit, as long as they
move **before** the unit does. From then on they are no longer part of it. If the unit can't move
that phase for any reason, the character can't leave it.

### How the joined unit works

- **Unit Mass.** For as long as the character is in the unit, add their Unit Mass (chapter 2) to the
  unit's.
- **Spells.** If the unit is under a spell that affects the whole unit, a character who joins it is
  affected too. If the character is under a spell that could affect a unit, the unit they join is
  affected too.
- **Movement.** The unit moves as one, the character with it, at the Movement of its **slowest**
  model. Any movement limit on the character or on the unit binds both. A M8 unit joined by a M4
  character moves 4". A character standing beside the unit must still be beside the front rank, and
  touching it, when the move ends.
- **Measuring.** Distances to or from the unit are taken from the whole unit, including a character
  standing beside it. Distances that belong to the character, such as its Command range, are taken
  from the character model.
- **Seeing.** The unit's Line of Sight and vision arcs come from the whole unit, a character beside
  it included. When the character itself must see (to cast a spell, for example), use the character
  model; its own unit does not block its view. Line of Sight **to** the character is drawn to the
  character model, and here its own unit may block it.
- **Fleeing.** If the unit flees, the character flees with it, even if a special rule would normally
  stop the character from fleeing. If the fleeing unit is caught and destroyed, the character dies
  too.

## Characters and shooting

**Firing.** A character with a missile weapon in a unit with missile weapons must pick the same
target as the unit. Each uses its own characteristics. This applies to missile weapons only, not to
spells such as magic missiles or vortexes.

**Being shot.** A character inside a unit of the same troop type (infantry with infantry, cavalry
with cavalry) can't be picked out by enemy shooting, except when:

- the attack is one that may target a single model inside a unit,
- the character is under a template, or
- the unit is down to **four or fewer** rank and file models.

In that last case, the owner first puts one hit on each rank and file model of the unit. The hits
left over are then split as evenly as possible between the unit and its character(s).

### Take the Blow!

When a shooting attack hits a character, and the unit still has at least five rank and file models sharing
the character's troop type, the character may call **Take the Blow!** Roll a D6:

| D6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | The hit strikes the character. |
| 2+ | A soldier steps in front: resolve the hit against the unit instead. |

## Characters in melee

A character in a unit always heads for the thick of the fight. In melee:

- **Moving up.** When their combat is picked (Step 1.1, the Choose Combat & Fight sub-phase,
  chapter 6), a character who is not in the fighting rank may move through the ranks into it. The
  inactive player's characters move first.
- **Striking.** A character in the fighting rank makes as many attacks as their St and their
  position against the enemy allow.
- **Choosing targets.** A character may aim all their attacks at, or share them out between, any
  enemy models in base contact with them.
- **Being struck.** Only attacks aimed at the character, by enemy models in base contact with them,
  can hit the character. This includes Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks.
- **Impact Hits and Stomp Attacks** may be aimed at the character only while the unit is down to four
  or fewer rank and file models.

A character beside the fighting rank counts as being in it.

**No spill-over.** Spare wounds caused to a unit never carry over onto a character in it. Spare
wounds caused to the character never carry over onto the unit.

### Moving through the ranks

Sometimes a character in a fighting unit has to **move through the ranks**. Pick the model up and put
it in the space you want, in or beside the right rank, shuffling the other models out of the way.

A character moved this way into any spot other than the front rank goes straight back to where it
was as soon as the unit is out of combat.

### Too many characters

The fighting rank may hold no more characters than rank and file models, unless the unit is only one
rank deep. Any extra characters must **retire** from the fight.

**Retiring.** A retired character:

- moves through the ranks to a spot away from the fighting rank,
- makes no attacks, and no attacks may be aimed at them, and
- gives the unit nothing: no Resolve, no special rules, no other benefit.

## Challenges

A challenge is a duel inside a melee, hero against hero. The winner can swing a whole battle.

### Issuing a challenge

Challenges are issued as the combat is picked, in Step 1.1 (Choose Combat & Fight sub-phase).

1. A combat allows **one** challenge.
2. The active player may issue it first. If they don't, the inactive player may.
3. The challenger must be a character or a champion (see command groups below) in or beside the
   fighting rank. A player with no such model can't issue a challenge.

### Accepting a challenge

The other player may accept by naming one of their own characters or champions that is in or beside
the fighting rank. With no such model, nobody can accept; if the enemy unit has no characters or
champions at all, the challenge simply goes unanswered.

### Refusing a challenge

If the other player turns the challenge down, the challenger's player picks one of the characters or
champions who could have accepted. That model retires at once (see Retiring above): it shuffles back
through the ranks, takes no part in the fight, and gives its unit nothing. It may not come back to
the fighting rank while its unit is still in combat with the model that issued the challenge.

**No way out.** A character or champion may **not** refuse when:

- they are not in a unit, or are the last model left in their unit, or
- their unit has enemies engaged in every one of its four arcs (completely surrounded).

### Fighting the challenge

Once accepted, the two models fight each other alone, in Reflexes order, aiming every attack at each
other. While the duel lasts, no other model in that combat may aim attacks at either of them.

If you can, move both models through the ranks so they touch. If that isn't practical, leave them
where they are.

**Mounts.** A mount of either duellist (including a chariot's crew) must aim its attacks at the other
duellist. If one duellist dies before a mount strikes, that mount's attacks are lost.

**Overkill.** When a duellist slays the opponent with unsaved wounds to spare, more than the
opponent had Hit Points left, each wound over counts **+1** to the Melee Outcome, up to **+5** in all. This
breaks the normal rule, where only Hit Points actually lost count towards the Melee Outcome.

**To the death.** If both duellists survive the round and the combat goes on into the next player's
turn, the challenge goes on too. Nobody may issue a new challenge in that combat until this one is
settled.

# Part two: Command groups

## The command group

Many units may buy a command group, as their army list allows. The **full command** is a champion, a
standard bearer and a musician. Some units can have only one or two of them. Each is bought as an
upgrade to one of the unit's rank and file models.

Command group models are still rank and file models for every rule that mentions rank and file,
unless a rule says otherwise.

The words are fixed whatever the model holds. A "standard bearer" may carry an icon, a pennant or a
totem; a "musician" may play a drum, a horn, a pipe or anything else. A champion often has a title
of its own in the army list, but it always follows the champion rules here.

### Where they stand

- Unless told otherwise, put the command group in the **front rank**, as near the middle as you can.
  The standard bearer takes the centre spot (or the nearest to it), with the rest of the group on
  either side.
- **Not enough room.** If the whole group won't fit in the front rank, put as many there as fit and
  the rest in the rank behind.
- **Make Way.** A unit that turns or reforms brings its command group back to the front. Once the
  unit's move is over, move its command group models into the new front rank.

## Champions

Most units may have one champion; a few may have more.

- **Characteristics.** A champion is usually a little better than the others in its unit. Its profile
  shows how.
- **Equipment.** A champion counts as having the unit's equipment, whatever the model carries. Some
  may buy extra kit that the rest of the unit can't; the army list says so where this applies.
- **Challenges.** A champion may issue and accept challenges just as a character can.
- **Shooting.** A champion fires at the unit's target, using its own characteristics.

### Champions as casualties

A champion is removed only in the cases below. When it is, put an ordinary rank and file model in its
place.

**From shooting,** a champion is removed only if:

- it is the last model left in the unit,
- the attack is one that may target a single model inside a unit, or
- it lies under a template.

**In melee,** a champion is removed only if:

- it is the last model left in the unit, or
- the attacks were aimed at it by enemy models in base contact.

Spare wounds caused to a champion never carry over onto its unit.

**In melee** a champion in the fighting rank fights normally, and any enemy model touching it may
choose to aim attacks at it.

**Take the Blow!** When a shooting attack hits a champion in a unit of five or more rank and file
models, roll a D6. On a 1 the champion is hit. On 2+ another model of the unit is hit instead.

## Standard bearers

A standard bearer has the same characteristics as the rest of its unit and counts as having the same
equipment, whatever the model actually holds.

**Melee bonus.** A unit with a standard bearer adds **+1** to its Melee Outcome (chapter 6).

**Losing the standard.** When the standard bearer falls, another model of the unit (not one from the
command group) picks the standard up. So the unit keeps its standard bearer until only it and the
champion (if any) are left. This holds even against attacks that may target a single model inside a
unit, and under a template. If the champion is the only other model left, the standard bearer is
removed before the champion.

### Trophies

A unit's standard becomes a trophy for the enemy when:

- the unit is caught and destroyed while fleeing, or
- the unit is wiped out in melee by an enemy unit.

Each trophy is worth extra victory points when the battle ends (see the scenario you are playing). A
standard taken this way can't be won back. A unit destroyed any other way, by shooting for example,
gives up no trophy.

## Musicians

A musician has the same characteristics as the rest of its unit and counts as having the same
equipment, whatever the model actually holds.

- **Quick time.** A unit with a musician that is 8" or less from an enemy unit and wants to march adds
  **+1** to its Resolve for the Resolve Test, up to Rs 10.
- **Steadying rhythm.** A fleeing unit with a musician adds **+1** to its Resolve whenever it tries to
  rally, up to Rs 10.
- **Onwards!** If the Melee Outcome is a tie, the side with a musician in the front rank of at least
  one of its units adds **+1**. If both sides have one, they cancel out and neither adds anything.

**Losing the musician.** When the musician falls, another model of the unit (not one from the command
group) takes up the instrument. So the unit keeps its musician until only the musician, standard
bearer and champion are left. This holds even against attacks that may target a single model inside a
unit, and under a template. If the champion and standard bearer are the only other models left, the
musician is removed before either of them.
