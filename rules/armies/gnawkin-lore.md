# Gnawkin: lore

Under every city in the Sundered Lands there is another city, and it is bigger. The people above hear it as scratching in the walls, a draught from a cellar that should not have one, grain that goes missing a sack at a time. The Gnawkin hear it as home. They have been digging beneath Ostara for longer than anyone on the surface has been writing things down, and they regard the surface folk as tenants who have not yet been told the rent is due.

## The Heart Seam

The Gnawkin believe the world is a fruit and they are eating it. Somewhere at its centre lies the Heart Seam, a vein of pure wyrdstone as large as a mountain, and there the Gnawing One waits to reward whichever of his children chews through first. Wyrdstone is everything to them: food, coin, fuel and sacrament. It glows green in the dark, it makes the clever cleverer and the mad madder, and a Gnawkin who eats too much of it tends to explode, which the others consider a fair price.

## The Market Below

There are no kings in the warrens, only the Market Below. Every rank, every warband, every tunnel and every life is for sale, and the great clans are trading houses as much as families. Clan Voltrik sells machines and lightning. Clan Blightfur sells disease and calls it faith. Clan Shadeclaw sells the deaths of rivals, and Clan Fleshwright sells monsters made to order. A warlord does not inherit an army; he outbids someone for it, and spends the campaign making sure the investment pays.

## The Swarm

A Gnawkin army is a crowd with a profit margin. Rankrats pour forward in vast, shoving blocks, kept honest by the Ironvermin behind them. Pale Seers call down Wyrd Lightning and read the Gnawing One's will in the smoke. Sewer Runners and Dusk Runners slip out of drains behind the enemy line, and Fumeslingers lob glass globes of choking gas at anyone foolish enough to stand still.

Voltrik's engineers bring the expensive toys: Wyrdlock Jezzails that punch through plate at long range, the crackling Sparkwheel, Wyrdfire Throwers and the Wyrd Lightning Cannon, which works most of the time. Blightfur's Blight Monks charge in a frenzy of sores and prayer. Fleshwright's handlers drive Ratbrutes into battle and, when the price is right, unleash a Warren Abomination, a heaving mountain of flesh that nobody fully controls. Rat Swarms boil up between everyone's feet.

## Why They Fight

The warrens are full. Every season there are more Gnawkin, and every season the tunnels must reach further. The surface was always going to be next. When the ground opens under a city in the Sundered Lands, the Gnawkin are not attacking. They are expanding the premises.
