# Sand Kings of Kheshet: lore

Far to the south of the Sundered Lands, past the last green coast, lies Kheshet: a country of red cliffs, white cities and a riverbed so dry that the fish bones in it have turned to stone. Nothing lives there now. Its kings are not living either, and they have not noticed that this is meant to stop them.

## The Long Noon

Kheshet was once the richest kingdom on Ostara, fed by the river Sefa and ruled by Sand Kings who were also priests of Sharu, the sun. Then the Sefa failed. The rains moved north and the people left for the coasts. The kings did not leave. Their priests told them the drought was the Long Noon, a single hot hour in the sun god's day, and that when it passed the river would return. A king who waited would wake to a green country and a grateful people.

So the kings waited. The Embalmers' Order wrapped them in linen and salt, sealed them in their tombs with their armies around them, and began the Ledger of Days, a count of every sunrise until the river came back. The Grave Priests still keep it. When an enemy disturbs a tomb, or the count reaches a date the Order thinks important, they wake their king and explain, very patiently, how long he has been asleep.

## Overdue Tribute

The trouble is that the Sand Kings remember everything as it was. Every kingdom of the Sundered Lands once paid tribute to Kheshet, or so the tomb walls say, and none of them has paid in three thousand years. King Amenkhet, the King of Kings, wakes more often than the others and is the angriest about it. When his host marches north it is not invading. It is collecting.

## The Host of the Dead

A Sand King's army is the army he was buried with, and it obeys him as it did in life. Skeleton Warriors and Skeleton Archers march in perfect ranks. Skeleton Horsemen ride on the flanks, and Skeleton Chariots, the pride of Kheshet, charge in great wedges of bronze and bone. The Sand Guard hold the line around the king and his Sand Princes.

Behind them come the works of the Tombwrights, the builders who carved the tombs and were buried in them: Tomb Scorpions that burrow up beneath the enemy, the Bone Colossus, Bonesphinxes with blades for claws and Bone Serpents that coil through the dunes. Screaming Skull Catapults hurl burning heads at walls built long after the skulls were cut. Tomb Swarms and Carrion follow.

## What They Fight For

The river has not come back. The Grave Priests know it. The kings suspect it. But a Sand King swore to wait for a green Kheshet, and until it returns he will take what he is owed, one overdue city at a time.
