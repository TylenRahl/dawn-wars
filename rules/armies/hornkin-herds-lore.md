# Hornkin Herds: lore

Ask a Concord woodcutter what lives in the deep forest and he will tell you not to go and find out. The Hornkin were walking the woods of Ostara before the first March was drawn on a map, and they do not believe the map.

## The Long Round

The Hornkin are a people who never stop moving. Each herd walks a great circle through the forest called the Long Round, a path so old that the trees have grown around it. A herd may take thirty years to walk it once, and its calves will walk it after. Along the way stand the Hornstones, great raw pillars that mark where the Round turns and where the herd must gather.

To the Hornkin the Round is not a road. It is a promise between the herd and the land. When they find a village built across it or a wall raised over one of its turns, they do not see settlers. They see thieves who have broken the promise, and they come through at night to set it right.

## Horns and Rank

A herd is ruled by horns, and by little else. Lesserhorns, small and quick, run at the edges and do as they are told. The Hornkin themselves are the body of the herd. Greathorns, the oldest and heaviest, guard the Hornchief and the banners. Over them all stands the Herdlord, who holds his place until someone with bigger horns takes it.

The Herdseers are the herd's memory. They read the Round in tree rings and in the Rotmoon, whose waxing calls the herds to war. When the Rotmoon is full, the Herdseer blows the Herdhorn, and every Hornkin within a day's run comes to the nearest stone.

## The Stampede

A Hornkin army does not advance. It arrives from three directions at once, out of trees that were empty a moment earlier. Lesserhorn skirmishers loose spears and vanish. Hornkin herds crash into the flank, Greathorns behind them swinging axes as long as a man. Hoofrunners gallop down the lines. Razorboar and Tuskboar Chariots plough through the ranks, and the Bull-hornkin, led by a Bloodbull, charge with a noise like a falling barn.

Behind them come the herd's monsters: the Gorgemaw that eats what it tramples, the one-eyed Gazehorn and the Gibberwyrm, which no two survivors describe alike. Greatest of the living herdlords is Korvash Ironhoof, whose Round runs through half the Concord's northern Marches.

## What They Want

The Hornkin do not want the Sundered Lands. They want them back the way they were. The Dominion Hosts sometimes pay herds to march beside them, and the herds take the plunder and walk off. No master rules the Long Round, the Hornkin say, and every herd that has followed one has come home with shorter horns.
