# Kingdom of Lyonne: lore

In the west of the Sundered Lands lies a country of green hills, white castles and slow rivers, and at its heart lies a lake that has never once frozen. The Lyonnese call it the Mere, and they say it is alive. Every song in Lyonne is in the end a song about water.

## The Long Flood

Seven hundred years ago the Mere rose. For three summers it crept over the fields and swallowed whole towns, while the quarrelling dukes of the west only watched. Then Arnaut the Founder, a minor lord with a borrowed horse, rode into the shallows and refused to come out. On the ninth day a woman in grey stepped from the water and asked him what he would give to have his country back.

Arnaut offered himself, and every knight after him. The Mere drew back to its old banks, and the bargain has held ever since. The woman in grey is the Lady of the Mere, and every knight of Lyonne owes her one year of his life, spent on the open road, keeping the springs and wells where her water rises.

## The Ladder of Vows

A young noble who has not yet served his year rides with the Knights Errant, loud and desperate to be noticed. Once he returns he joins the Knights of the Lily, the steady backbone of every Lyonnese host, who charge in a tight wedge of lance and banner. Some never come home. They become Wayfarer Knights, sworn to the road until the Lady calls them back, and they fight with great blades and little patience.

Rarest of all are the Chalice Knights. The tale says a Chalice Knight once carried a single cup of Mere water across a dying province and poured it on the dust, and the rain followed him home. Above them all, the Verdant Knight is seen only at the oldest springs, and only when one of them is threatened.

## The Hands of the Kingdom

The knights win the songs, but the land is held by its people. Every fief owes the crown a levy, and the levy has a right the knights do not: if a lord breaks faith with his village, the village may name his replacement. So the Men-at-Arms hold their pikes with some pride, the Peasant Bowmen loose their shafts in long grey clouds, and Mounted Yeomen ride the flanks. Battle Pilgrims follow the holiest knights with relics on their backs. Field Trebuchets hurl stone from the rear, and Pegasus Knights from the high crags fall on the enemy from the sky.

## What They Fight For

Damsels and the Prophetess read the Mere's will in still water and bring the Favour of the Mere to the battle line. The Lyonnese believe that if the vow is ever broken, the water will rise again. Every charge they make is made to keep it in its banks.
