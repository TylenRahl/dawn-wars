# Broken Crowns: lore

South of the Concord Realm and east of Lyonne, the maps of the Sundered Lands simply give up. The land there is a jumble of dry hills, stolen castles and roads that change hands twice a season. The people who rule it are the Marchlords, and every one of them wears a crown that somebody else is wearing too.

## Where Crowns Go to Break

Nobody is born in the Broken Crowns with a right to anything. They arrive. Second sons with no inheritance, knights who lost a duel they should have won, captains whose employer forgot to pay, a wizard chased out of a city for asking the wrong question. Each one comes south with a sword, a few loyal friends and a story, and if the story is good enough, more friends follow.

The custom here is called the Spring Count. On the first warm morning of the year every Marchlord stands in his own courtyard and counts his soldiers out loud, by name, in front of all of them. A prince who can pay every name keeps his crown for another year. A prince who cannot usually finds that his own captain has a crown of his own by supper. Prince Ravel the Twice-Crowned lost his throne this way, waited one winter, and bought it back from the man who took it.

## The Hired Host

A Marchlord's army is whatever he can afford this year. Sellswords form its hard core, professional and unsentimental, who fight well for exactly as long as the coin lasts. Around them stand the Freeholder Militia, farmers and townsmen defending their own fields, who fight harder than anyone thinks they will. Realm Archers, drifted south from the north, loose volleys from behind them, and Marchlord Brigands sneak along the hedges.

Hedge Knights ride for their own reasons, often for glory and more often for dinner. Hireling Outriders scout the flanks. The Might of Varezza rolls in from the coast: Marchlord Bombards, Marchlord Mortars and Organ Guns bought by the barrel from the foundries of that loud harbour city. Outcast Wizards, too odd or too honest for any college, pull on the Eight Threads in exchange for being left alone.

## Ogres and Giants

The richest Marchlords hire the biggest help. Crownsworn Ogres march in step, a little proud of their polished breastplates. Scorchlands Ogre Bulls come up from the burning south for meat and pay. Now and then a Hillcrusher Giant is bribed into the line, and every soldier near it learns to shout "Look Out Below!" at the right moment.

## What They Fight For

The Broken Crowns fight for the oldest thing there is: a place to call their own. The great kingdoms call them bandits. The Marchlords answer that every great kingdom started with a bandit who held on, and they mean to hold on longer.
