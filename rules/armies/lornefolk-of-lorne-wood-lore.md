# Lornefolk of Lorne Wood: lore

Kin to the Ilthari and so to Void Wars' Athanatoi; the Lornefolk keep the Grey Years at bay the way the Athanatoi later do, with the Hunt.

East of the sea, in the middle of the Sundered Lands, there is a forest that every map leaves blank. Rivers bend away from it. Roads stop at its edge. Woodcutters who walk in come out, if they come out at all, many years older than when they left. This is Lorne Wood, and the people who live inside it are the Lornefolk.

## The Rootfast

The Lornefolk were once Ilthari sailors, wrecked on a far shore and lost inland. Hungry and few, they came to the edge of the forest and asked it for shelter. The forest answered. It would hide them, feed them and fight for them, and in return every one of them would belong to it, body and memory, for as long as a tree stood in Lorne.

That bargain is the Rootfast, and it binds them still. When a Lornefolk child is born, a seed is planted in their name. When they die, they are laid beneath that tree, and their memories pass into its rings. The old trees of Lorne Wood know everything their people ever knew, and on quiet nights the Lornefolk walk among them to listen.

Two of the wrecked sailors never died. Amarys, who first spoke with the forest, and Hernath, who first fought for it, have stood at its heart ever since, slowly turning from flesh to bark and back again with the seasons. She is the wood's voice. He is its anger.

## The Hunt

Like all their kin, the Lornefolk do not age, and they fear the Grey Years, the slow fading of joy that comes with too many centuries. Their answer is the Hunt. Each spring they ride out under the horn of Talorn, the hunter god, and chase whatever has crossed into the wood that should not have, and the fear and thrill of it keep their hearts young.

## The Wood at War

A Lornefolk army is the forest itself, moving. Archers in green and brown loose arrows from cover that no enemy saw until it was too late. Pathwatchers and Pathstalkers, who have not slept under a roof in a thousand years, pick off captains from a mile away. Wilddancers, sworn to Lirrin the dancing god, spin through the enemy ranks painted and half-naked, led by a Leafsinger or a Gloomdancer. Riders on swift steeds, hawks and Great Eagles hunt the flanks.

And when the Lornefolk are pressed hard, the trees wake. Dryads step out of the trunks with fingers like thorns. Barkwraiths lead them, and behind them come the oldest trees of all, walking on their roots, each carrying the memories of a thousand dead.

## What They Fight For

The Lornefolk care nothing for kingdoms. Their whole world is the wood, and their whole law is the Rootfast. Any axe raised against Lorne is an axe raised against their grandmothers.
